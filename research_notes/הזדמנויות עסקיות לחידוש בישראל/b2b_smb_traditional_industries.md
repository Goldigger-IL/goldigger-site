## 1. Which sectors have the largest documented productivity or price gap versus OECD peers?

### Takeaway
The best-documented gaps are in construction (productivity roughly 36–54% below reference countries, depending on source and year), followed by trade, food and accommodation services, which the Bank of Israel names as the largest negative contributors to Israel's overall productivity gap. Up-to-date sector-by-sector OECD comparisons for 2024–2026 were not retrievable in this session; the freshest numbers found are Taub Center's 2025 comparison against five small European economies.

### Cited Findings
**Economy-wide**
- [OFF] OECD Economic Survey of Israel (April 2025) describes a "dual economy": a highly productive high-tech sector alongside traditional low-productivity sectors that employ most of the workforce; limited competition and stringent product-market regulation hinder productivity gains; main recommendation is removing barriers to domestic and foreign trade (red tape, border processes, standards harmonisation, tariffs). — [OECD executive summary](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-israel-2025_d6dd02bc-en/full-report/executive-summary_8df11c0f.html); [OECD press release](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2025/04/oecd-economic-surveys-israel-2025.html)
- [OFF] Same survey: investment at end-2024 was about 15% below its pre-war level, held back by labour shortages "especially in construction" after Palestinian work permits were suspended. — [OECD executive summary](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-israel-2025_d6dd02bc-en/full-report/executive-summary_8df11c0f.html)
- [OFF] The survey's cost-of-living chapter ties high prices to Balassa-Samuelson effects (high-tech wages pushing up non-tradable prices) plus weak competition. Specific price-gap percentages were not retrieved. — [OECD cost-of-living chapter](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-israel-2025_d6dd02bc-en/full-report/addressing-the-high-cost-of-living_bfe408a2.html)
- [OFF, undated in extract, probably 2019] Bank of Israel: GDP per hour worked in Israel is 24% below the OECD average. — [Bank of Israel press release](https://boi.org.il/en/communication-and-publications/press-releases/increasing-the-standard-of-living-in-israel-by-increasing-labor-productivity)
- [OFF, undated in extract] Bank of Israel: productivity gaps in food and accommodation services, construction and trade make the greatest negative contribution to the overall gap. — [Bank of Israel press release on domestic-market vs export industries](https://boi.org.il/en/communication-and-publications/press-releases/labor-productivity-in-domestic-market-oriented-and-export-industries-an-analysis-from-an-international-perspective/)
- [OFF/think-tank] Taub Center State of the Nation 2025: versus Austria, Denmark, Finland, the Netherlands and Sweden, the services value-added-per-worker gap narrowed from 19% to 7% between 2015 and 2023; per hour worked the services gap is about 28%, and the gap in industry stayed around 40%. Comparison group is five countries, not the OECD average. — [Taub Center SNR 2025 macro chapter](https://www.taubcenter.org.il/en/research/snr-2025-macro/)
- [OLD, 2014–2015] Taub Center: Israeli productivity was below nearly all of eleven developed OECD countries in four of five business sectors; the largest shortfalls were in wholesale and retail trade, repairs, hotels and restaurants, transport and construction; non-tradable services were the main contributors to the widening gap in 1995–2009, with five large industries responsible for 81% of the widening. — [Taub Center, low productivity](https://www.taubcenter.org.il/en/research/low-productivity-a-systemic-problem-across-israels-economy/); [Taub Center, industry-level comparison](https://www.taubcenter.org.il/en/research/causes-of-the-widening-productivity-gaps-between-israel-and-the-oecd-a-multiyear-industry-level-comparison/)

**Construction**
- [EST, 2025] Technion/EY construction-innovation course material: productivity in Israeli construction is only about 64% of the average in reference countries. — [Technion innovation centre PDF](https://innovation.technion.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ניהול-חדשנות-בענף-הבנייה-קורס-2-גרסה-להפצה-5-7-25.pdf)
- [OLD, 2014] Taub Center: construction productivity almost 40% below the OECD average, linked to heavy reliance on unskilled foreign workers. — [Taub Center](https://www.taubcenter.org.il/en/research/low-productivity-a-systemic-problem-across-israels-economy/)
- [PRESS, date unclear] Bank of Israel-based report: construction productivity 54% lower than the comparison average while economy-wide productivity is 11% lower. — [Jerusalem Post](https://m.jpost.com/business-and-innovation/real-estate/article-792848)
- [OFF] Bank of Israel/Ministry of Construction paper names low labour productivity as a core challenge of the sector. — [Bank of Israel paper](https://www.boi.org.il/media/thchtn0j/מאמר-7-דארין.pdf)

- [EST] ConTech community presentation (Dec 2025): about 39 months average to build a residential apartment. Not an official statistic. — [Technion ConTech presentation](https://innovation.technion.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/מצגת-ConTech.pdf)

**Food prices and produce margins**
- [OFF] Inter-ministerial margins committee, as covered by Calcalist: when produce goes through the wholesale market, the farmer accounts for 57% of the consumer price, the wholesaler 18%, the retailer 25%; the same coverage also gives 64% for farmers under another calculation. — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/rjt2bthzq)
- [EST, opinion piece] Average fruit-and-vegetable intermediation margin of 154% attributed to a CBS report. — [Maariv opinion](https://www.maariv.co.il/journalists/opinions/article-871561)

- [OFF] State Comptroller (Oct 2025): identical products cost 20.6%–130.3% more through commercial import than through personal import. — [State Comptroller Oct 2025](https://library.mevaker.gov.il/sites/DigitalLibrary/Documents/2025/2025-10/2025.10-001-HE.pdf)
- [PRESS] A retail survey found a 263% price spread for the same cucumber across retailers. — [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/economy/consumerism/article-1352286)

**Skills**
- [OFF] OECD Education at a Glance: 20% of Israelis aged 18–24 were NEET in 2025 (17% in 2015) versus an OECD average of 13%; only 3% of 25–34-year-olds hold an upper-secondary vocational qualification versus 8% of 55–64-year-olds. — [OECD EAG Israel note](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/education-at-a-glance-2026_c0e523a9-en/israel_b61ec67f-en.html)

### Inferences
- Construction is the single sector where (a) the gap is largest, (b) every source agrees on direction, and (c) a war-driven labour shock adds urgency. The three figures (36%, ~40%, 54% below) use different years and comparators and should not be averaged.
- The produce chain's inefficiency is less about the wholesaler's cut (18%) than is commonly claimed; the retailer's share (25%) and retail price dispersion (263% for one item) are larger. A pure "cut out the wholesaler" pitch is weaker than a price-transparency or direct-to-small-retailer logistics pitch.

### Gaps
- OECD 2025 survey figures on Israel's price level versus the OECD average, by category, were not retrieved (oecd.org blocked; search budget exhausted).
- No 2024–2026 Bank of Israel table of labour productivity by industry versus OECD was retrieved; the Bank of Israel 2025 annual report chapters exist ([chapter 2](https://boi.org.il/media/uttds3su/chap-2-2025.pdf), [chapter 8](https://boi.org.il/media/xnlno4nq/chap-8-2025.pdf)) but were not read.
- The Knesset Research Center estimate of wholesale and retail shares in produce ([document](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/25/Committees/25_cs_mmm_2714004.pdf)) was located but its numbers were not extracted.
- No productivity or margin benchmark found for logistics, security, cleaning, property management, clinics or kashrut.

## 2. Which regulatory or structural shocks in 2023–2026 force businesses to change behaviour now?

### Takeaway
The strongest forced-adoption event is the Israel Invoices (חשבוניות ישראל) allocation-number regime, whose threshold fell from NIS 25,000 (May 2024) to NIS 5,000 (1 June 2026), pulling nearly every B2B invoice into real-time digital clearance. Second-tier shocks are the construction and agriculture labour shortage since October 2023, reconstruction and compensation money, the Wolt Market divestiture order (Feb 2026), Privacy Amendment 13 enforcement, cash-use limits, and the retail electricity opening.

### Cited Findings
**Israel Invoices (allocation numbers)**
- [OFF/vendor] Threshold path: NIS 25,000 from May 2024; NIS 20,000 from January 2025; NIS 10,000 from 1 January 2026; NIS 5,000 from 1 June 2026. - [PRESS] Without an allocation number the buyer cannot deduct input VAT. — [Kipa explainer](https://www.kipa.co.il/כדאי-לדעת/1232040-0/)
- [OFF via PRESS] Tax Authority research estimated that in 2025 transactions worth about NIS 12.2–16.4 billion were shifted below the reporting threshold — the stated rationale for lowering it. Exact article among the two links not confirmed. — [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/general/news/article/20033277); [Walla](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3814340)

### Inferences
- At NIS 5,000 the allocation-number rule reaches ordinary SMB-to-SMB invoices, so any business still on paper or desktop invoicing has had to move to a connected system during 2026. The window for winning those switchers is largely the 12–18 months around the June 2026 step; by late 2026 most have already chosen a vendor.
- The Wolt Market divestiture and a possible Uber Eats entry are the first real opening in restaurant delivery since 2020, but they change grocery quick-commerce more than restaurant commission levels.
- Reconstruction money is real but slow and politically contested (the frozen NIS 1 billion), which favours operators with working capital over those relying on prompt state payment.

### Gaps
- No finding on open banking for businesses, the accessibility regulations, or the "what is good for Europe" import reform (searches did not run or returned nothing).
- No confirmation of any further threshold step after NIS 5,000 or of a move to full mandatory e-invoicing for all invoices.
- Electricity opening terms for small businesses specifically were not found.
- Whether the Wolt decision has been appealed or implemented is unknown.

## 3. In each sector, who are the incumbents, how old is their technology, and how able are they to respond?

### Takeaway
Back office is the one sector with strong, well-capitalised, recently consolidated incumbents (Morning under TeamSystem, Priority under Blackstone, Hyp under Max, Hilan). Most other sectors — property management, produce wholesale, food-service supply, kashrut, clinics, security and cleaning — are fragmented with little evidence of dominant software, but also little published data on size, so the "open field" reading is partly an absence-of-evidence finding.

## 4. Which local-market Israeli B2B startups succeeded, and what revenue scale is realistic with about 700,000 businesses?

### Takeaway
Documented local-market outcomes cluster at roughly $100–150 million exits for horizontal SMB software (Morning) and several hundred million dollars for mid-market ERP (Priority, about $800 million on trackers), while payroll and HR leader Hilan shows a domestic B2B services company can approach NIS 3 billion in revenue. No disclosed revenue was found for Morning, Tabit, Hyp or Bllink, so realistic revenue ranges rest on inference.

### Cited Findings
- Morning: about 150,000 active business customers; Fortissimo invested $20 million in 2021 at about $50 million valuation; reported $150 million sale to TeamSystem in Dec 2024 (price from anonymous sources; not officially disclosed). — [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/s1sss8hb1e); [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=234477)
- Priority Software: Blackstone majority deal May 2024, terms undisclosed, about $800 million on deal trackers; one aggregator lists revenue of $301 million and EBITDA of $40 million with no year given (single unverified source). Priority earlier bought hotel-software firm SilverByte for a reported NIS 120 million (another report: about $35 million). — [Mergr](https://mergr.com/the-blackstone-group-acquires-priority-software); [multiples.vc](https://multiples.vc/private-comps/priority-software); [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/h1q9sb5mme)
- Hyp: card issuer Max rolled up Caspit, CreditGuard, Yaad Sarig and EZcount into one SMB payments-and-invoicing brand; no prices found. — [Start-Up Nation Central](https://finder.startupnationcentral.org/company_page/ezcount)
- Tabit: over $100 million raised; Dealroom valuation band $100–250 million (2022). No exit, no revenue. — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/tabit/)
- Hilan: NIS 2.84 billion revenue in 2024. — [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/tlv/HLAN/financials/)
- Bllink: $1.6 million raised (2021); no later round found. — [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/articles/0,7340,L-3911934,00.html)
- Counter-example: Melio, founded by Israelis for B2B payments, sold to Xero for up to $3 billion — but its market is the US. — [Israel Desks](https://israeldesks.com/?p=8048)
- Market ceiling: about 711,000 active businesses (2024), 731,600 (2025). — [CBS](https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/mediarelease/DocLib/2025/250/29_25_250b.pdf); [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/economy/israel/article-1350420)

### Inferences
- Morning reached roughly one in five Israeli businesses (150,000 of about 711,000). If average revenue per customer is NIS 70–120 a month (my assumption, not sourced), revenue would be about NIS 125–215 million a year; the $150 million price would then be roughly 2.5–4.5 times revenue. Treat as an order-of-magnitude check only.
- A horizontal SMB software product in Israel therefore tops out around NIS 150–250 million revenue even with market leadership. Higher revenue needs either take-rate on money flows (payments, credit — Hyp's logic for Max), mid-market ticket sizes (Priority), or labour-heavy services (Hilan, whose total includes IT services, not only payroll).
- A vertical product for a 10,000–15,000-business niche (restaurants, clinics, kashrut-supervised venues) at NIS 500–1,500 a month caps at roughly NIS 60–270 million at full penetration, so realistic revenue is in the low tens of millions unless payments or supply are layered on.
- Buyers of local winners have been foreign strategic or private-equity owners (TeamSystem, Blackstone) and domestic financial groups (Max); exit sizes found are $35–150 million for single products.

### Gaps
- No audited revenue for any private company named. Morning's revenue, Tabit's Israeli revenue and restaurant count, Hyp's merchant count and Beecomm's status were not found.
- No IVC or Start-Up Nation Central aggregate on local-market B2B exits was retrieved.

## 5. Realistic entry costs and achievable moats

### Takeaway
No source gives entry-cost figures by sector; the only hard anchors are funding histories (Bllink ran on $1.6 million; Morning scaled on a $20 million growth round; Tabit consumed over $100 million with hardware and overseas expansion) and state grant terms for modular construction (30–50% of development costs). Sector ranges below are my own estimates and should be presented as such.

### Cited Findings
- Software anchors: Bllink $1.6 million total; Morning $20 million round at about $50 million valuation; Tabit $105–131.5 million total. — [CTech](https://www.calcalistech.com/articles/0,7340,L-3911934,00.html); [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/?p=234477); [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/tabit)
- Asset-heavy anchor: modular-construction grant of NIS 11 million shared by two companies, covering 30–50% of approved development costs — implying pilot programmes of roughly NIS 11–18 million each. — [Nadlan Center](https://www.nadlancenter.co.il/article/7562); [Israel Hayom](https://www.israelhayom.co.il/business/real-estate/article/13057960)
- Licence as barrier: 792 licensed non-bank credit providers, yet 18 listed firms hold about 47% of the book. — [ICE](https://www.ice.co.il/finance/news/article/1133885)
- Regulatory integration as barrier: invoicing software must connect to the Tax Authority allocation-number service. — [gov.il](https://www.gov.il/he/service/request-assignment-number-for-tax-invoice)
- Platform conflict risk: the regulator treated Wolt's dual role (marketplace plus own retailer) as a structural conflict. — [Walla](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3810424)

### Inferences (researcher's estimates, not sourced)
| Sector | Software/platform entry | Operating/asset-heavy entry | Most plausible moat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Construction, modular, renovation | NIS 3–10 million | NIS 50–200 million for a factory; less for a renovation/reconstruction contractor | Scale and approvals; weak for software alone |
| Back office (invoicing, books, payroll) | NIS 5–15 million plus heavy acquisition spend | n/a | Switching costs and accountant channel; incumbents strong |
| SMB credit and factoring | NIS 3–8 million plus licence | NIS 50 million+ lending capital | Data, licence, cost of funds |
| Food-service and kiosk supply | NIS 3–8 million | NIS 10–40 million (warehouse, fleet, stock) | Route density, supplier terms |
| Restaurant operations | NIS 5–15 million | n/a | Switching costs; delivery network effects sit with Wolt |
| Property management | NIS 2–6 million | NIS 2–10 million roll-up of small firms | Payment flows, multi-year contracts |
| Licensing, kashrut, privacy compliance | NIS 1–4 million | Service firm under NIS 2 million | Low; regulatory know-how |
| Clinics and dental | NIS 3–8 million | n/a | Patient-record switching costs |
| Security, cleaning, manpower | NIS 2–5 million | Working capital for payroll, NIS 5–20 million | Scale and tender track record; thin margins |
- The 47% share held by 18 lenders despite 792 licences suggests a licence alone is not a moat; funding cost and scale are.
- Sectors with money flows attached (credit, supplier payments, building-committee dues, restaurant procurement) offer the only path past the roughly NIS 200 million revenue ceiling inferred in section 4.

### Gaps
- No sourced capital-requirement data for any sector (factory cost, minimum equity for an expanded credit licence, fleet costs, customer-acquisition cost).
- No evidence gathered on incumbents' actual churn or contract lengths, so switching-cost claims are untested.
