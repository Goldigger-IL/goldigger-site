## Key question 1 — Which sectors show the largest, best-documented price or margin gap versus comparable countries?

### Takeaway
The best-documented gaps are in **food and consumer packaged goods** (overall consumption prices 29% above the OECD average in Nov 2024; a press headline puts food and beverages 52% above; three suppliers hold 85% share in 20 categories) and in **banking** (five big banks earned about NIS 32.1bn in 2025 at 15–17% return on equity). **Hotels** show the largest single headline gap (rooms 75–130% dearer than Europe) but hoteliers report lower, not higher, margins than abroad, so that gap is a cost problem more than an excess-profit one. Insurance distribution (NIS 13.2bn of agent commissions in 2025) and car importing (about NIS 5bn combined importer profit in 2022) are large profit pools, but with weaker international-comparison evidence in this session.

### Cited Findings

#### Economy-wide price level
- Israel's consumption price level in November 2024 was 29% above the OECD average; fruit and vegetables were the only category cheaper than the OECD average (Knesset Research and Information Center review, January 2025) — [Knesset RIC PDF](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/globaldocs/MMM/d463eb34-11cd-ef11-a856-005056aa1f91/2_d463eb34-11cd-ef11-a856-005056aa1f91_11_20790.pdf)
- OECD Economic Survey of Israel (April 2025): Israel's price level is among the five highest in the OECD, with food and housing among the highest, and is higher than its GDP per capita would predict. Causes named: tariffs, cumbersome border procedures and red tape that raise import prices and reduce competitive pressure, plus strict product-market regulation. The OECD also cautions that high prices are not by themselves proof of distortions — [OECD survey chapter "Addressing the high cost of living"](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-israel-2025_d6dd02bc-en/full-report/addressing-the-high-cost-of-living_bfe408a2.html); [OECD Ecoscope blog, 2 Apr 2025](https://oecdecoscope.blog/2025/04/02/why-is-life-so-expensive-in-israel/); [OECD press release](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2025/04/oecd-economic-surveys-israel-2025.html)
- Counter-view: the Histadrut argues the OECD's liberalisation recipe "has been tried for a decade and failed" and that the missing piece is regulation of concentrated markets such as food, banking and real estate — [Histadrut opinion](https://global.histadrut.org.il/?p=3250)

#### Food retail, food manufacturing and food/toiletries importing

- The ten leading food retailers and the ten largest consumer-goods suppliers each hold about 50% or more of their field — [Walla](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3853104) (attribution uncertain)
- Shufersal (שופרסל): net profit NIS 735m in 2025, up about 10.5% from NIS 665m; gross margin about 30%, described as a record; the chain "stopped chasing market share and chose profitability", with record profitability alongside a sharp sales decline (IBI analysis) — [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/economy/consumerism/article-1305846); [ICE](https://www.ice.co.il/finance/news/article/1108700) (attribution uncertain)

- Strauss: Q1 2026 operating profit NIS 316m, up 67.9% year on year; added to the list of "concentrated entities" after 2025 turnover crossed the statutory threshold for the first time — [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/general/news/article/20035670)
- Private label: State Comptroller data show private label reached only 6.9% of food and consumer-goods sales in 2022, one fifth of the European average. Chains' own figures are higher: Rami Levy 26.44% of sales in 2025; Shufersal 18.1% in 2025 and 20.6% in Q1 2026. Carrefour group globally: 38% of food sales in 2025 — [Walla](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3859624) (attribution uncertain; alternative [Walla](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3805333))
- Counter-evidence on causes: a Reichman University conference presentation states that VAT and kashrut together explain about 70% of the food price gap — [Reichman presentation (Shahar Turgeman)](https://www.runi.ac.il/media/neqfbuc2/מצגת-שחר-תורגמן.pdf) (attribution between two Reichman decks uncertain; other deck: [Dr Sarit Menahem-Carmi](https://www.runi.ac.il/media/ijlfwrpn/מצגת-דר-שרית-מנחם-כרמי.pdf))

- Reform rationale used by government: an average family pays more than NIS 10,000 a year extra for the products covered by the "what is good for Europe" reform — [JDN](https://www.jdn.co.il/economy/2247111/) (attribution uncertain)
- Imported produce mark-ups: retail chains were found charging more than double (153% above) what they pay for imported grapes — [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/retail-chains-found-to-be-charging-over-double-what-they-pay-for-imported-grapes/amp/); [FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/latin-america/article/9831944/israel-reviews-grape-duties-as-retail-prices-exceed-import-costs-153/)
- Competition Authority sanctions and consent decrees since the start of 2023 total more than NIS 158m, of which NIS 88m in food (cases included Tnuva and Osem) — [ICE](https://www.ice.co.il/law/news/article/1019593)

### Inferences
- Ranking by quality of evidence for an "excess rent" (my judgment): (1) food manufacturing/importing and CPG — official sources (Knesset RIC, State Comptroller, OECD) agree on both the price gap and the concentration; (2) banking — the profit numbers are official and large, though I found no session source that benchmarks Israeli net interest margins against the OECD; (3) insurance distribution — NIS 13.2bn of commissions is a very large intermediary take for a population of about 10 million, but I have no international ratio; (4) car importing — high absolute profits in 2021–2022, but 2025 net margins of 2.5%–4% at listed importers suggest the rent has already been competed down by Chinese brands and inventory gluts.
- Carasso's NIS 327m net profit over 51,861 vehicles is about NIS 6,300 net profit per vehicle (my arithmetic from the two figures above; includes non-sales income such as leasing, so it overstates per-car sales profit).
- Good Pharm's reported ~9% share of a NIS 6.7bn market is about NIS 600m, which matches its reported 2025 sales — the two numbers corroborate each other.
- Hotels look like a "high price, low margin" sector: a new entrant with the same cost base (land, kashrut, security, arnona, labour) would not earn excess profit simply by undercutting; the opening is in cost-structure innovation (limited-service, non-kosher or self-catering, modular construction), not in a margin grab. Demand risk is extreme (occupancy 27%–53% in 2025–2026).
- The food debate has two rival explanations with different implications for an entrant: if concentration and exclusive importers drive the gap (State Comptroller, Strum), parallel importing and private label can capture margin; if VAT and kashrut explain about 70% (Reichman deck), the capturable margin is far smaller. Both are cited above; I could not verify the methodology of either.

## Key question 2 — Where did regulation recently open a door (2023–2026), and has anyone already walked through it? Who, with what results?

### Takeaway
Three doors opened widest: **household electricity supply** (about 486,000 households and businesses had switched by roughly September 2026, through seven consumer brands owned mostly by telecom and gas incumbents), **banking licences** (a tiered-licence law passed in 2026; Revolut's Israeli bank was approved for establishment on 10 November 2026; Esh is still rolling out) and the **"what is good for Europe" import reform** (it worked for electrical goods but, per Globes in May 2026, food imports are "stuck" and food concentration is unchanged). The food/toiletries import door is therefore open on paper and still largely un-walked.

### Cited Findings

#### Import reform — "מה שטוב לאירופה טוב לישראל"
- The reform consists of four laws; the fourth passed its second and third readings, completing it. It lets food, cosmetics/toiletries, electrical goods and standard-bound products (bicycles, baby strollers) be imported without Israel-specific adaptations, and lets anyone import from any supplier any product sold in Europe — which is expected to expose international brands that today arrive only via exclusive importers — [JDN](https://www.jdn.co.il/economy/2247111/); [Walla](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3854196) (attribution uncertain)
- For food and toiletries the importer declares that the product is sold in Europe, with no prior state inspection; press noted there is no guarantee that importers pass savings on to consumers — [Ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/economy/article/hkx94kvjc)
- Result after more than a year (Globes, May 2026): import data show a competitive turnaround in electrical appliances, but in food concentration is unchanged and consumer prices are rising; part of the difficulty is attributed to Health Ministry regulation — [Globes import tag page](https://www.globes.co.il/news/%D7%99%D7%91%D7%95%D7%90.tag) (headline: "יותר משנה לרפורמת היבוא: שוק החשמל נפתח לתחרות, אך יבוא המזון נתקע")
- Further steps: a baby-products stage in the second half of 2026; a reform approved by the Knesset in July 2026 covering strollers, toys and cleaning materials, with the change reaching consumers at the start of 2027 — [Srugim](https://www.srugim.co.il/101011263-עגלות-צעצועים-וחומרי-ניקוי-השינוי-שיגיע)
- A separate "what is good for America" import-recognition reform was also approved by the Knesset — [JDN](https://www.jdn.co.il/economy/2678842/) (attribution uncertain)
- The Competition Authority stepped up enforcement on parallel imports (Globes headline, 26 August 2026; body not available) — [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001553484)
- Incumbent retailers are using the door themselves: Shufersal plans to import meat directly from South America (Globes, 30 June 2026) — [Globes import tag page](https://www.globes.co.il/news/%D7%99%D7%91%D7%95%D7%90.tag)
- Government also moved to ease import rules for global retail chains — [The Media Line](https://themedialine.org/headlines/israel-to-ease-import-restrictions-for-global-retail-chains/print)
- The Knesset cancelled a planned increase in the VAT exemption on personal imports — [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001535894)

#### Banking licences, open competition law, payments
- Before One Zero (licence 12/2022) and Esh Bank Israel (company formed 09/2019), no new bank had been licensed in about 40 years; a joint Finance Ministry–Bank of Israel team was appointed in November 2024 — [Bank of Israel box on the tiered-licence outline](https://www.boi.org.il/media/icthv5bt/תיבה-בנושא-מתווה-למתן-רישיון-בנק-מדורג-והחוק-לקידום-התחרות-בשוק-הבנקאות.pdf)
- November 2025 draft law (Law for Promoting Competition in the Banking Market): tiered licensing with a "tiny bank" (up to 2.5% of system assets) and a "small bank" (up to 5%), moving tiers only after two consecutive years above the threshold; temporary exemptions include online account-switching (3 years), fee-tariff publication flexibility (5 years) and the executive pay cap (10 years) — [Gornitzky client memo](https://www.gornitzky.co.il/wp-content/uploads/pdf/post/29414_----------.pdf)
- The law is referred to by the Bank of Israel as enacted: "Law for Promoting Competition in the Banking Market (Legislative Amendments), 5786-2026" — [Bank of Israel press release, 6 May 2026](https://boi.org.il/publications/pressreleases/6-5-26/)
- The Supervisor of Banks issued Proper Conduct of Banking Business Directive 490, "Supervisory framework for small and new banks", applying Basel proportionality by size tier — [Bank of Israel press release](https://boi.org.il/media/kwtkdnwq/מסגרת-פיקוחית-לבנקים-קטנים-וחדשים-הודעה-לעיתונות.docx); outline page: [Bank of Israel](https://boi.org.il/roles/supervisionregulation/gradual_outline/)
- Who walked through: in May 2026 the Governor approved Revolut Payments Israel moving through the licence stages and let Esh Bank advance on access to the payment systems; on 10 November 2026 the Bank of Israel approved the establishment of "Bank Revolut Israel (in formation)". ## Key question 3 — Which past Israeli disruptions succeeded and which disappointed or failed, with numbers and reasons?

### Takeaway
The 2012 cellular reform is the benchmark success for consumers (prices cut by about 55%, industry revenue down 38% in four years) but a mediocre outcome for the disruptor's investors — Golan Telecom was sold for NIS 350m and then NIS 590m and the market has since re-consolidated with rising ARPU. Format imports that copied a foreign brand without a cost advantage did badly (7-Eleven lost NIS 68m and exited within about 18 months), while disruptors that attached themselves to a real cost or distribution advantage did well (Good Pharm from 3% to about 9% share under Rami Levy; Libra in motor insurance). The new digital bank One Zero has about 200,000 customers after a cumulative loss of about NIS 1bn and its controlling shareholder is reportedly exploring a sale.

### Inferences
- Lessons from the cellular reform (my synthesis of the cited facts): (a) the disruption needed regulatory scaffolding, not only a licence; (b) a 50%+ price cut on a simple, standardised monthly subscription moved hundreds of thousands of customers within a year; (c) the consumer surplus was permanent enough (revenue pool down NIS 6.5bn a year by 2014) but the entrant's equity value was modest and ended in sale to an incumbent; (d) the state blocked the first exit to an incumbent (2015) and allowed it five years later, so "build to sell to an incumbent" carries regulatory risk; (e) a decade later the market is re-concentrating and ARPU is rising, i.e. disruption windows re-open.
- The same pattern is repeating in electricity: a simple percentage discount on a regulated tariff, marketed by firms that already bill the household. This suggests that "regulated price minus X%" is the proposition Israeli consumers respond to fastest.
- Successful challengers shared a structural cost or distribution advantage: Rami Levy/Good Pharm (existing discount logistics and brand), Libra (13% expense ratio), telecom groups in electricity (existing billing base), Chinese car makers (product cost). Failed or weak challengers imported a brand or format without one: 7-Eleven (10 stores, NIS 68m annual loss), Be.
- One Zero's economics: about NIS 1bn cumulative loss for about 200,000 customers is roughly NIS 5,000 of loss per acquired customer (my arithmetic), with annualised revenue of about NIS 130m, or roughly NIS 650 per customer per year. A full-service bank built from scratch has so far been an expensive way to attack bank rents; the non-bank lenders took far more share (17 points of consumer credit) with narrower products.
- Carrefour is a mixed case: a rebrand of a distressed 140-store chain, now reported operating-profitable (about NIS 86m in H1 2026) and cited as the biggest price-cutter in one survey, but valued at only NIS 750–850m and with no evidence in this session that it changed market-wide food prices (food inflation still ran 1.5 times the CPI in 2024–2026).

## Key question 4 — What does it realistically cost (capital, licences, time) to enter each sector at a meaningful scale versus a niche?

### Takeaway
I found almost no primary-source figures for licence capital requirements or entry budgets in this session, so the ranges below are mostly inference from observed transactions and losses, and should be presented as such. The observed data points say: a from-scratch full bank costs on the order of NIS 1bn and 5+ years; a national retail chain is worth and costs high hundreds of millions of NIS; asset-light "reseller" models on regulated infrastructure (electricity supply, mobile virtual operators, parallel importing, digital insurance agencies) are one to two orders of magnitude cheaper but have thin gross margins and depend on the regulator's wholesale terms.

### Cited Findings
- Bank, full build: One Zero accumulated about NIS 1bn of losses to reach about 200,000 customers and still targets breakeven at end-2026, about five years after it began onboarding and four after its 12/2022 licence date cited by the Bank of Israel — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/article/sm8qduv9g); [Bank of Israel](https://www.boi.org.il/media/icthv5bt/תיבה-בנושא-מתווה-למתן-רישיון-בנק-מדורג-והחוק-לקידום-התחרות-בשוק-הבנקאות.pdf)
- Bank, time to licence: Esh's company dates from 09/2019 and it had still not fully launched in 2026; Revolut's UK banking licence took about 20 months of preparation and came only in March 2026; in Israel Revolut went from stage approval (May 2026) to establishment approval (10 November 2026) — [Globes](https://globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001562731); [Bank of Israel](https://boi.org.il/publications/pressreleases/6-5-26/)
- Bank, new lighter route: the 2026 law creates "tiny" (up to 2.5% of system assets) and "small" (up to 5%) bank tiers with multi-year exemptions (account-switching 3 years, fee tariff 5 years, pay cap 10 years), and Directive 490 scales supervisory requirements by size — [Gornitzky](https://www.gornitzky.co.il/wp-content/uploads/pdf/post/29414_----------.pdf); [Bank of Israel](https://boi.org.il/media/kwtkdnwq/מסגרת-פיקוחית-לבנקים-קטנים-וחדשים-הודעה-לעיתונות.docx)
- Mobile operator, value of a scaled challenger: Golan Telecom (about 900,000 subscribers) changed hands for NIS 350m and then NIS 590m plus about NIS 130m debt forgiveness; HOT Mobile was bid at NIS 2.1–2.3bn; Wecom at NIS 265m — [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-cellcom-signs-binding-agreement-to-buy-golan-telecom-1001318927); [Gadgety](https://www.gadgety.co.il/?p=370294); [Gadgety](https://www.gadgety.co.il/?p=365648)
- Supermarket chain, value at scale: Carrefour Israel, about 140 branches and about NIS 3.4bn annualised revenue, is being valued at NIS 750–850m — [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/capitalmarket/news/article/20046848)
- Convenience format, cost of a failed niche entry: 7-Eleven lost about NIS 68m in one year with 10 stores and recovered NIS 3.36m on exit — [Ynetnews](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/hjmubj4va)
- Drugstore chain, scale needed for relevance: Good Pharm needed 68 branches to reach about 9% share and about NIS 600m sales; the Sapir group's new "Pharm Plus" is starting with a 25-branch target; a chain is "large" for regulatory purposes at NIS 277m turnover (proposed NIS 450m and 12 branches) — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/market/article/bkyheuxygg); [Globes tag](https://www.globes.co.il/news/%D7%A8%D7%A9%D7%AA%D7%95%D7%AA_%D7%A4%D7%90%D7%A8%D7%9D.tag); [ICE](https://www.ice.co.il/consumerism/news/article/1072471)
- Electricity supplier: 22 suppliers were active by October 2025, which indicates a low licensing barrier; the scarce input is discounted energy allocated by regulator tender (500 MW in the second tender, about 5% of it reaching households) — [Noga](https://www.noga-iso.co.il/media/0f3l30vy/סטטוס-ניודים-למספקים-פרטיים-102025.pdf); [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/energy/news/article/20024398)
- Car importing: 85 active parallel importers by July 2026 indicates a low licensing barrier, but they moved only about 6,700 cars in 2025; official importers of Chinese brands carry large unsold inventories — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/hyzv6fmsbe); [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/breaking-news/article-1254762)
- Food/toiletries importing: under the reform an importer's declaration that the product is sold in Europe replaces prior inspection — [Ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/economy/article/hkx94kvjc)
- Insurer: Libra reached profitability and a 13% expense ratio seven years after its 2018 start — [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/marketopionion/news/article/20029798)

### Inferences
(All ranges in this subsection are my own order-of-magnitude estimates derived from the cited data points and general knowledge; none is a sourced figure.)
- **Full digital bank:** NIS 0.7–1.5bn and 5–8 years to breakeven (One Zero is the only data point). **Niche under the new "tiny bank" tier or a payment/credit licence:** plausibly NIS 50–200m and 2–3 years, but no entrant has yet completed the tiered route, so this is untested. Partnering with an existing licence holder (the Isracard–Esh logic, although that deal fell through) shortens time.
- **Non-bank consumer credit / card-linked lending:** the segment that actually took share (banks 88% → 71%). Entry needs a Capital Market Authority credit licence plus funding lines; capital need scales with the loan book (tens of millions of NIS for a niche book, hundreds of millions to matter). Credit-loss risk is rising (Discount's loan-loss expense nearly doubled in 2025, per key question 1 sources).
- **Electricity supply (virtual supplier):** low single-digit to low tens of millions of NIS for licence, guarantees, billing system and customer acquisition, 6–12 months; gross margin is the spread between the tender/bilateral energy price and the 5%–21% customer discount, so it is a scale-and-marketing game that favours firms with an existing customer base. A stand-alone start-up would likely need a distribution partner (retailer, bank, municipality, employer).
- **Parallel importing of food/toiletries:** niche entry is cheap (a few million NIS of working capital per container line, weeks to months) now that declaration-based import exists; meaningful scale requires guaranteed shelf space, which means either owning a retail format or a supply contract with a discount chain (Rami Levy, Yochananof, Carrefour, Good Pharm) or selling direct online. I would budget NIS 20–100m for a branded multi-category parallel-import house with its own logistics, and much more (NIS 300m–1bn) for a physical hard-discount chain of 30–70 stores, by analogy with Good Pharm's branch count and Carrefour's valuation.
- **Drugstore discount chain:** 25–70 branches to matter; at a rough NIS 2–5m per branch fit-out and stock, NIS 60–300m. Pharmacy licensing and pharmacist staffing are the operational bottleneck, and Super-Pharm's advantage lies in prescriptions and location.
- **Digital insurer:** insurer licence capital plus reinsurance and 5–7 years to scale (Libra path) — likely NIS 100–300m; **digital agency/comparison platform:** low millions of NIS but dependent on insurers' willingness to pay commissions to a channel that compresses prices; the possible 2027 move to customer-paid advice fees would change this channel's economics entirely.
- **Car retail:** official import of a new (Chinese) brand needs showrooms, service network and inventory finance — hundreds of millions of NIS; parallel import is licensable cheaply but has stayed at about 2% of the market, which implies non-licence barriers (warranty, parts, homologation paperwork, manufacturer obstruction, financing).
- **Mobile/telecom:** a virtual operator is cheap (low tens of millions) but ARPU of NIS 46–48 leaves little margin; this market is past its disruption phase.
- **Hotels:** capital-intensive per room and currently facing 44%–53% occupancy; entry at scale is a real-estate play, not a disruption play.
- **Fuel retail:** regulated pump price and margin for 95-octane leave little room for price disruption; no data to size entry.

### Gaps

- Licence conditions and guarantee amounts for an electricity supplier without generation; the wholesale energy price available to such suppliers: not sourced.
- One Zero's total capital raised (as opposed to cumulative loss): not found.
- Actual store-opening costs for supermarkets and drugstores in Israel, customer acquisition costs in electricity and insurance, kashrut certification costs for imported lines: not found.

## Key question 5 — Are incumbents hostile and able to block entry (lobbying, exclusivity, control of shelf space, kashrut, standards)?

### Takeaway
Yes, with documented cases: exclusive importers lobbied a limiting clause out of the Arrangements Law, insurance agents have repeatedly stalled remuneration reform and threatened a High Court petition, the State Comptroller blames three ministries for under-implementing import reforms, and the state itself has blocked consolidation moves in both directions. The strongest non-regulatory moat is consumer inertia and trust (Super-Pharm, the big banks), and the commonest end-state of a successful challenger is being bought by an incumbent.

### Cited Findings
- Exclusive importers: "the importers lobbied and the clause limiting their power disappeared from the Arrangements Law" (Calcalist caption referring to Schestowitz, importer of Colgate; undated in the summary) — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/hk5aacwcs)
- Behind-the-scenes pressure over opening imports of coffee, salt and dozens of other products (Globes headline: "who is pressing behind the scenes?") — [Globes](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001423490)
- At a Knesset State Control Committee hearing the chair said importers earn abnormal profits because of concentration; the State Comptroller criticised the Economy, Finance and Health ministries for ineffective implementation of import reforms — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/shopping/article/b1iimgzo11g)
- Health Ministry regulation is named as a reason food imports are "stuck" one year into the reform; the ministry replied that prices are not its responsibility — [Globes import tag](https://www.globes.co.il/news/%D7%99%D7%91%D7%95%D7%90.tag)
- Kashrut is cited both as a cost driver of food prices (with VAT, about 70% of the gap in one presentation) and as a reason European private-label ranges cannot simply be copied to Israel ("the local consumer judges each range by taste, kashrut and availability") — [Reichman presentation](https://www.runi.ac.il/media/neqfbuc2/מצגת-שחר-תורגמן.pdf); [Walla](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3805333) (attribution uncertain)
- Insurance agents: the agents' association threatened a High Court petition over the commission claw-back mechanism when the regulator moved against over-priced life policies (Maariv, likely an older item); in 2026 the Capital Market Authority "acceded to the request of the Insurance Agents Association" on one matter and the association welcomed the draft circular on life-insurance discounts; the Finance Ministry's customer-pays model is being "returned to the table" for 2027, i.e. it was shelved before — [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/economy/israel/article-684901); [Funder](https://funder.co.il/article/212466); [Mako/N12](https://www.mako.co.il/news-money/2026_q2/Article-c2ada8e9a69de91026.htm)
- Agents and insurers steered clients back into double-coverage health policies after the reform (regulator warning; 40% reportedly returned to the dearer policy) — [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/general/news/article/20021812); [Globes tag](https://www.globes.co.il/news/%D7%A8%D7%A4%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%9E%D7%AA_%D7%91%D7%99%D7%98%D7%95%D7%97%D7%99_%D7%94%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%90%D7%95%D7%AA.tag)
- Incumbent contracts as a brake on switching: in the El Al frequent-flyer card move from Cal to Isracard, Cal's existing agreement runs to 31 December 2026 and Cal argued that approaching existing customers earlier than three months before expiry (October 2026) breaches the agreement — [ICE](https://www.ice.co.il/finance/news/article/1113517) (attribution uncertain; alternative [ICE](https://www.ice.co.il/finance/news/article/1116757))
- Consumer inertia: many shoppers buy at the leading drugstore chain even when they know cheaper alternatives exist, as a matter of security and trust — [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/economy/israel/article-1234931)
- The state as a blocker and enabler: the Antitrust Authority, backed by the Prime Minister and Finance Minister, blocked Cellcom's NIS 1.17bn purchase of Golan in 2015 to keep the maverick alive, then the deal was signed again in 2020 at NIS 590m; in 2026 Victory needs Competition Authority clearance even to buy 5%–10% of Carrefour — [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-pm-antitrust-authority-oppose-cellcom-golan-merger-1001116808); [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/capitalmarket/news/article/20046848)
- Enforcement backing for challengers: Competition Authority sanctions of more than NIS 158m since 2023 (NIS 88m in food) and stepped-up enforcement on parallel imports in August 2026 — [ICE](https://www.ice.co.il/law/news/article/1019593); [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001553484)
- Incumbents co-opt the opening: Shufersal's own direct meat import; telecom and gas incumbents as the main private electricity brands; Pelephone's bids for HOT Mobile and Wecom — [Globes import tag](https://www.globes.co.il/news/%D7%99%D7%91%D7%95%D7%90.tag); [TheMarker guide](https://www.supermarker.themarker.com/bills/guides/private-electricity-suppliers-guide); [Gadgety](https://www.gadgety.co.il/?p=370294)
- Hoteliers argue the problem is state-imposed cost, not their margins (bureaucracy, kashrut, arnona, security); David Fattal has said that competition is what would bring prices down — [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/airlines/news/article/20043610); [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3742569,00.html) (attribution uncertain)

### Inferences
- Hostility ranking (my judgment from the above): highest in insurance distribution (an organised lobby of about 13,000 agents with a NIS 13bn income stream and a record of stalling reform) and in CPG importing (documented lobbying plus control of brand supply); high in banking (not through lobbying evidence in this session, but through inertia, account-linked products and incumbent-owned payment apps); moderate in food retail (chains compete on price with each other but control shelf space, and the top retailers increasingly import directly themselves); low in electricity supply (the incumbent utility is not the commercial rival, and the regulator sets targets for switching).
- A challenger whose model depends on an incumbent's cooperation (shelf space from a big chain, commissions from insurers, card-scheme contracts) is exposed; the documented successes either owned the customer interface (Golan, Rami Levy/Good Pharm, Libra) or rode a regulator-mandated wholesale arrangement (electricity).
- Exit to an incumbent is likely to be scrutinised early in a challenger's life and tolerated later (Golan 2015 vs 2020; One Zero's possible sale will test this again in banking).
