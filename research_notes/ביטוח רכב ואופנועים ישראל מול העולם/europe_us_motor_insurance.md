## 1. Average annual motor premium per vehicle or policy, 2023-2026, by country; relation to income and purchasing power

### Takeaway
Mandatory third-party liability in continental Europe costs roughly EUR 130-440 a year per car (Poland lowest, Italy highest), and an average insured vehicle with all covers costs about EUR 400-650; the UK average comprehensive policy was GBP 564 in 2025. The United States is not cheap: the regulator-based average spend was USD 1,282 per insured vehicle in 2023 and quote-based "full coverage" averages were USD 2,100-2,700 in 2026, so the "American drivers pay much less" half of the claim is contradicted by the data.

## 2. Claims frequency and cost; road deaths per population and per vehicle-kilometre (including Israel)

### Takeaway
Israel's road-death rates (about 4 per 100,000 residents and 5 per billion vehicle-km in 2023) sit inside the Western European range (2-5 and 3-5), so the "similar accident rates to Europe" premise holds for fatalities; the US is roughly three times worse per capita (about 12-13) and nearly double per kilometre. Insurance claim frequencies differ widely between countries (liability claims per 100 vehicles: about 3 in France and the US, 5-6 in Germany, Italy and Poland, about 10 in the UK on older data), so "similar claim rates" cannot be assumed from similar death rates.

## 3. Insurer profitability in motor lines 2021-2025: loss, expense and combined ratios; return on equity

## 4. Structural differences that drive price (liability system, limits, who pays medical costs, legal environment, uninsured driving, theft)

### Takeaway
A premium buys different things in different countries: the US mandates very low liability limits (typically USD 25,000 per injured person) but the motor insurer pays medical bills and litigation-inflated settlements, while European compulsory cover has very high or unlimited limits but public health and social-security systems absorb much of the injury cost. No-fault design, legal environment and uninsured-driver rates (15.4% in the US) explain most of the cross-state and cross-country spread.

## 5. Motorcycles: premiums, what is covered, fatality share

### Takeaway
Motorcycle liability is cheap in the US (roughly USD 150-360 a year liability-only on one insurer's data) and comparable to a car's liability premium in Italy (EUR 355 against EUR 432 for cars), and in fault-based systems it does not cover the rider's own injuries. Riders are about 16-20% of road deaths in both the EU and the US while travelling a tiny share of kilometres, so a system that does insure the rider's own injury must charge far more.

- EU fatality share: riders of powered two-wheelers were 20% of road deaths in 2024 (car occupants 44%); in 2023 motorcyclists 17% and moped riders 2%; cars cover up to 35 times the passenger-kilometres of two-wheelers. [search-excerpt] — [European Commission, 18 March 2025](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/eu-road-fatalities-drop-3-2024-progress-remains-slow-2025-03-18_en); [ERSO powered two-wheelers report](https://www.efa-eu.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/8-ERSO-TR-Powered-two-wheelers-20240912.pdf)
- US: 6,228 motorcyclist deaths in 2024 (6,366 in 2023) on 9.26 million registered motorcycles: 67.25 deaths per 100,000 registered motorcycles and 28.00 per 100 million vehicle-miles; 86,887 injuries (938 per 100,000 registered motorcycles). In 2023 the occupant fatality rate per 100 million vehicle-miles was 31.39 for motorcycles against 1.13 for passenger cars; per 100,000 registered vehicles 66.57 against 11.61. Helmet use 74% (2023). [page-read] — [Triple-I motorcycle crashes](https://www.iii.org/fact-statistic/facts-statistics-motorcycle-crashes)

### Inferences
- US motorcyclists were 6,228 / 39,345 = 15.8% of road deaths in 2024; per mile travelled a rider is about 28 times more likely to die than a car occupant (31.39 / 1.13). [inference]
- US annual risk per registered motorcycle: about 0.067% death and 0.94% injury. If a compulsory policy had to fund the rider's own injury costs (medical care, lost earnings, disability), the pure premium would be dominated by that roughly 1% annual injury probability times a five- to six-figure average cost, which is why first-party rider cover is excluded from cheap liability policies abroad. [inference]
- A low foreign motorcycle premium therefore mostly reflects that the rider's own body is uninsured by that policy (covered instead by health insurance, social security or nothing), not better statistics. [inference]

### Gaps
- No reliable average motorcycle liability premium found for Germany, France, Spain, the Netherlands, the UK or Poland (only content-farm ranges such as EUR 120-180 for third-party-only in the EU and GBP 150-500 for a 125cc machine in the UK; not usable).
- Whether personal-injury-protection states in the US extend first-party injury benefits to motorcyclists (several exclude motorcycles) was not retrieved. UK motorcyclist fatality share not retrieved.

## 6. New entrants and "better statistics" plays: results of digital, telematics and data-driven motor insurers

### Takeaway
Almost every data-driven motor start-up lost money for many years: Root needed about a decade from founding and nine-figure cumulative losses before its first annual profits (2024-2025); Metromile, Friday and Elephant ended in distressed sales after hundreds of millions invested; Tesla Insurance still pays out more in claims than it collects (loss ratio about 100-115%). The few profitable ones (Marshmallow, Prima, Admiral, Zego recently) won through cost discipline, underserved niches and a hard pricing market, not by proving that incumbents overcharge.

### Cited Findings
- Root (US, telematics-priced, listed 2020): 2019 loss ratio about 100% (Q3 2019: 113%; Q4: 93%); 2020 direct loss ratio 76% (helped by pandemic driving declines and USD 10 million favourable reserve development); "seasoned" states ran 15 points better than unseasoned states in H2 2020; its annual report states renewal premiums carry lower loss ratios than first-term premiums. An IPO-era analysis observed that Root "should expect lower-than-average losses because it selects better drivers, but its loss results didn't suggest this". [search-excerpt] — [Carrier Management](https://www.carriermanagement.com/features/2020/06/09/207674.htm); [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2021/03/04/603635.htm); [Coverager](https://coverager.com/insurtech-root-insurance-2020-ipo-unpacked/)
- Root 2024-2025: net income USD 30.9 million (2024) and USD 40.3 million (2025, record); net combined ratio 96.4% (2024) and 98.2% (2025; Q4 2025 99.7% against 91.5%); gross written premium USD 1.5 billion (+16%); 481,869 policies in force (414,862); sales and marketing spend USD 175 million (USD 136 million in 2024); fastest growth via independent agents; management expects lower net income in 2026 because new policies carry higher loss ratios. [search-excerpt] — [Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/02/26/859701.htm); [Coverager](https://coverager.com/root-reports-q4-2025-results/)
- Metromile (US, pay-per-mile): loss ratio in the low 70s through 2019 against a market average of 64; 57.3% in 2020 (operating loss USD 37.4 million); back to 81.6% in Q3 2021 and 85.1% accident-year in one 2021 quarter; accumulated deficit USD 246.5 million (end-2019) and USD 366.6 million (end-2020). ### Inferences
- Time to profitability (founding years are my recollection, not sourced here: Root 2015, Zego 2016, Friday 2017): Root about 9 years to its first profitable year (2024); Zego about 8 years to its first profitable month (December 2024); Elephant 15 years (2009 to 2024) to a profitable year, then sold; Metromile and Friday never. [inference]
- Loss ratios in early years ran 70-115% against 60-75% for incumbents. The recurring mechanism is adverse selection plus the "new business penalty": a new insurer that quotes below the market attracts precisely the customers whom incumbents (with more data) priced higher, and first-term policies run worse than renewals in every book (Root says so explicitly). Under-pricing incumbents is therefore the fastest route to the worst risks. [inference]
- Customer acquisition is a material cost: Root spent USD 175 million on sales and marketing in 2025 while net policies grew by about 67,000 (roughly USD 2,600 per net added policy; gross new policies and hence true acquisition cost per policy are not disclosed). [inference]
- Successful entrants found a segment where incumbents demonstrably misprice (Marshmallow: migrants with foreign driving records) or a distribution cost advantage (Prima, Admiral, Linea Directa), and benefited from the 2023-2024 hard market. None demonstrates that an average driver can be insured "much cheaper". [inference]

### Gaps
- No results retrieved for Clearcover, By Miles, Cuvva, Getsafe's motor book, Progressive Snapshot's measured loss-ratio lift, or Direct Line's telematics models. My recollections, unverified in this session: Metromile was valued above USD 1 billion at its 2021 listing and was bought by Lemonade in 2022 for roughly USD 150-200 million in shares; Root lost well over USD 1 billion cumulatively in 2019-2023; By Miles was acquired by Direct Line in 2023.
- Per-policy customer acquisition cost was not found for any of these insurers.
- Prima's loss ratio and audited profit, and Marshmallow's audited 2025 accounts, were not found.

## 7. Transferability of foreign loss statistics and the role of reinsurers in supporting start-up motor insurers

### Takeaway
No evidence was found that foreign loss statistics can price another country's motor risk: claim frequency and average cost differ by a factor of two to three between neighbouring European countries, and even an expert insurer (Admiral) lost money for 15 years after carrying its model to the US. Reinsurers do fund start-ups through quota-share treaties, but they take a proportional share of the same losses, became more selective after 2022-2024, and do not supply a pricing advantage.

### Cited Findings
- Cross-country heterogeneity (2016): liability claims per 100 vehicles ranged from 2.4 (Finland) and 3.1 (France) to 5.8 (Germany), 7.3 (Portugal) and about 10 (UK, 2013); average liability claim from EUR 1,412 (Portugal) and EUR 1,741 (Poland) to EUR 5,856 (France); liability risk premium from EUR 67 (Sweden) and EUR 89 (Poland) to EUR 226 (Germany) and EUR 268 (Italy). [page-read] — [Insurance Europe motor database](https://www.insuranceeurope.eu/downloads/european-insurance-industry-database-motor-insurance-statistics/Database-Motor.xlsx)
- Within one country, US state averages differ by about 2.7 times and bodily-injury claim behaviour depends on state law (no-fault states are excluded from the national bodily-injury series because they are not comparable). [page-read] — [Triple-I](https://www.iii.org/fact-statistic/facts-statistics-auto-insurance)
- Even within one company, "seasoned" states with approved models and a year of own data ran 15 points of loss ratio better than newly entered states (Root, H2 2020). [search-excerpt] — [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2021/03/04/603635.htm)
- Cross-border entry record: Admiral's Elephant (US) and Baloise's Friday (Germany/France) as in section 6; Admiral said the Elephant sale lets it focus on the UK and mainland Europe. [search-excerpt] — [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/ftse-100-insurance-giant-admiral-sells-us-business-elephant/); [Finews](https://www.finews.com/news/english-news/64965-baloise-friday-digital-insurance-merger)
- Practitioner view on entry pricing without own claims data: a new entrant can price off competitors' market prices, but "market premiums reflect what competitors charge rather than true losses" and the entrant "may be copying competitor pricing errors"; industry aggregate statistics are real claims data but "too coarse and lagged for entry pricing". (Actuarial blog, not peer-reviewed.) [search-excerpt] — [Burning Cost: market-based ratemaking](https://burning-cost.github.io/2026/03/25/market-based-ratemaking-no-claims-history); [Burning Cost: entry pricing](https://burning-cost.github.io/2026/04/01/entry-pricing-market-data-methods-uk-mga)
- Reinsurer support: quota share is the standard structure for full-stack start-ups (Marshmallow cedes proportionally, concentrated with one reinsurer; Bluefire, a US non-standard auto agency, cedes about two-thirds, USD 250 million, of premium); Gallagher Re reported that Munich Re and Swiss Re cut participation in agency-written business by over 50% in 2024, while a later report shows assumed agency premium up 84% at Hannover Re and 74% at Munich Re in 2025 (the two reports are not reconciled); casualty quota shares tie up reinsurer capital for long periods, limiting appetite. [search-excerpt] — [Gallagher Re via iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/2534434938); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/d430ed70-576c-4a08-8ec5-c210cffa9111); [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/reinsurers-are-more-involved-in-the-creation-of-mgas-becoming-insurers-risk-capital-partners-pro-mga-global-solutions-maleary/)
- Examples of reinsurer-backed motor distribution: Ageas Re's EUR 500 million quota share with Triglav for Prima-distributed motor business; Lemonade reducing its ceded share as results improved. [search-excerpt] — [Reinsurance News](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/ageas-re-in-e500m-quota-share-with-triglav-linked-to-prima-distributed-motor-business/); [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/reinsurance/news/breaking-news/insurtech-lemonade-scales-back-reinsurance-cession-550315.aspx)

### Inferences
- Foreign statistics transfer the method (rating variables, telematics scoring, model structure) but not the level: frequency, severity, legal rules, medical-cost allocation, repair prices and fraud patterns are local, and the harmonised European data show two- to threefold differences between adjacent markets. A reinsurer's foreign portfolio data can inform relativities (for example young versus old drivers) but the base rate must come from local claims. [inference]
- A quota-share treaty lowers the capital a start-up needs but passes the same loss ratio to the reinsurer, who prices the ceding commission on expected local results and will reduce or withdraw capacity if the book runs above about 100% combined (as happened across the sector in 2022-2024). Reinsurance is a financing tool, not evidence that the business can be priced lower. [inference]
- For the claim under test: similar death rates (true for Israel versus Western Europe) do not imply similar insured claim cost per vehicle, because what the policy must pay for differs; the correct test is local pure premium (claims paid per insured vehicle) against local premium, i.e. the loss ratio, which abroad sits at 75-92% with combined ratios near or above 100%. [inference]

### Gaps
- No documented case was found of an insurer successfully launching in a new country on foreign actuarial data alone, and no academic study on cross-country transferability of motor loss statistics was retrieved.
- No public terms (ceding commissions, loss corridors) of Munich Re, Swiss Re or Hannover Re quota-share treaties with named motor start-ups were found; Munich Re's known role as a backer of digital insurers could not be sourced in this session.
- Whether Israeli market statistics (the central rating database available to Israeli insurers) would already give a new local entrant the needed base data is outside this note's scope.
