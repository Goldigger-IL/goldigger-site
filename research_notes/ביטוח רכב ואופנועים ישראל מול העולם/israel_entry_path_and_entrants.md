## 1. Licensing: legal basis, process, capital, solvency regime, timing, fit-and-proper

### Takeaway
A full insurer licence is obtainable: four new non-life insurers were licensed in 2018–2024 (Libra and WeSure in 2018; Anchor and Securitas in August 2024) after the entry capital floor for a general insurer was cut in 2017–18 from NIS 59m to NIS 10m and the required control core from 50% to 30%. The floor is not the binding constraint — the Solvency II-based SCR (100% since year-end 2024, no phase-in left for new business) and the regulator's vetting of owners, business plan and systems are; the two 2024 licences took about two years. I found no dedicated "small/digital insurer" licence tier introduced or proposed in 2020–2026.

### Cited Findings
**Legal basis**
- Insurers are licensed under the Supervision of Financial Services (Insurance) Law, 5741-1981 (חוק הפיקוח על שירותים פיננסיים (ביטוח), התשמ"א-1981); a "foreign insurer" (מבטח חוץ) licence exists under s.15(a)(2); a separate licence type under s.15(a1) is carved out of the solvency circulars; capital rules are issued under s.35(b)–(d) after consulting the advisory committee and notifying the Knesset Finance Committee. [P] — [CMISA Codex, Gate 5 Part 2 Ch.2 "Insurer equity"](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/information-entities-codex/he/Codex_nm.1.pdf); [CMISA circular 2020-1-15](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/dynamiccollectorresultitem/solvency151020/he/Solvency.SolvencyII151020.pdf)
- Control: under s.32 no one may control an insurer (or a corporate agent) without a permit (היתר שליטה) from the Commissioner, on conditions he sets. [S] — [search excerpt; candidate URL Funder](https://www.funder.co.il/article/169253)
- Two capital layers exist by law: (a) minimum equity to obtain a licence, set by the Finance Minister in the Supervision of Financial Services (Insurance) (Minimum Equity Required for an Insurer Licence) Regulations, 5778-2018, which replaced the 1998 minimum-capital regulations; (b) equity required for solvency, set by the Commissioner. [P] — [CMISA Codex](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/information-entities-codex/he/Codex_nm.1.pdf)

**Minimum capital to get a licence**
- 2018 easing as reported when Libra was licensed (9 May 2018): general (non-life) insurer NIS 10m (previously NIS 59m); life insurer NIS 15m (previously 52m); composite NIS 25m (previously 89m); required control core cut to 30% from 50%; the Authority "eased the threshold conditions and shortened and streamlined the licensing process". [P] — [Calcalist, 9.5.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3737718,00.html); repeated in [Calcalist, 25.6.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3740980,00.html)
- For the August 2024 licences (Anchor, Securitas) Calcalist reports that each owner "was required to deposit an initial guarantee of NIS 15m to receive the licence" (ערבות התחלתית של 15 מיליון שקל). [P] — [Calcalist, 5.8.2024](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/rkcja7ct0). **Conflict/unclear:** NIS 10m (2018 reports) vs NIS 15m (2024 report); I could not open the regulations to see whether lines such as compulsory motor/liability carry a higher floor or whether the amount was indexed/changed.
- Practice at launch: Libra's founder reportedly put up about half the funding — "about NIS 20m including equity and set-up costs" — and "tens of millions of shekels in total were put into the company". [P] — [Calcalist, 10.5.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3737776,00.html). Bizportal instead puts her initial investment at ~NIS 8.8m and total at ~NIS 13m. [P] — [Bizportal, 21.6.2021](https://www.bizportal.co.il/capitalmarket/news/article/793607). **Conflict flagged.**

**Ongoing capital — the old "accounting" regime (משטר כושר פירעון חשבונאי)**
- Applies to an insurer not (yet) under the economic regime; required equity is the highest of: (a) the licence minimum; (b) a premium basis — 10% of general/health premiums written in the last 12 months up to a NIS 82m premium ceiling, 5% above it, plus 7.5% of retained premiums; (c) a claims basis — 20% of retained outstanding claims up to NIS 450m and 12% above; plus add-ons. [P, formula as extracted from the PDF; the ceilings were CPI-updated by circular 2020-1-16, so current amounts may differ] — [CMISA Codex](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/information-entities-codex/he/Codex_nm.1.pdf)
- Example of this regime on a start-up: at 31.12.2020 WeSure Insurance had equity surplus of ~NIS 12.6m over the requirement, an "accounting solvency ratio" of ~136% (rising to ~150% after a NIS 5m injection), on 2020 gross premiums of ~NIS 238m of which NIS 67.6m retained. [P] — [ICE, 1.4.2021](https://www.ice.co.il/finance/news/article/815608); [Bizportal, 1.4.2021](https://www.bizportal.co.il/general/news/article/790932)

**Ongoing capital — the economic (Solvency II-based) regime**
- Insurers in Israel have had to comply with the economic solvency regime, based on the EU Solvency II directive with local adaptations, since 31.12.2017 (original circular 2017-1-9 in force 30.6.2017). [P] — [Commissioner's Report 2025, ch.3](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%203_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf); [circular 2020-1-15](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/dynamiccollectorresultitem/solvency151020/he/Solvency.SolvencyII151020.pdf)
- Transitional rules (circular 2020-1-15, 14.10.2020): required capital could be 75% of SCR on Dec-2019 data, 80% (Dec-2020), 85% (Dec-2021), 90% (Dec-2022), 95% (Dec-2023) and **100% of SCR from Dec-2024 data onward**; a separate deduction from insurance reserves for long-term products sold in the past runs off linearly from 100% (Dec-2019) to 0% (Dec-2032) with Commissioner approval. The MCR is calculated on the SCR timetable. Excluded from the circular: s.15(a1) licensees, government insurers, and foreign insurers meeting reg. 3(b) of the 2018 minimum-equity regulations for their Israeli business. [P] — [circular 2020-1-15](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/dynamiccollectorresultitem/solvency151020/he/Solvency.SolvencyII151020.pdf)
- From the calculation as of 31.12.2026, solvency-ratio reports are to be published together with the periodic financial report (IFRS 17 alignment). [P] — [draft insurance circular 2025-23, dated 7.6.2026](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/dynamiccollectorresultitem/regulation-legislation347/he/2025-57.pdf)
- Sector solvency at 31.12.2025 (with transitional measures): aggregate economic capital ~NIS 94bn vs required ~NIS 59bn, ratio 160% (+2 pts); the "other insurers" group (IDI, AIG, Shlomo, Shomera, Libra, WeSure, Bituach Haklai, David Shield, credit insurers) rose 12 points year on year. [P] — [Commissioner's Report 2025, ch.3](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%203_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf)
- Individual ratios: Libra 145% at Dec-2020 and 254% after its June 2021 IPO [P] — [Bizportal, 30.6.2021](https://www.bizportal.co.il/capitalmarket/news/article/793945); Direct Insurance (IDI) ~134% at 31.12.2025 after dividends [P] — [ICE, 28.5.2026](https://www.ice.co.il/finance/news/article/1114707); Shlomo 118% (108% a year earlier), capital surplus ~NIS 140m [P] — [Bizportal, 20.8.2026](https://www.bizportal.co.il/Insurance/news/article/20039703); Ayalon 137% at 31.12.2025 and est. 150% at 31.3.2026 [P] — [ICE, 24.8.2026](https://www.ice.co.il/finance/news/article/1126915).
- The MBI operator lists "cost of required capital (Solvency II), cost of capital trapped in prudence margins in reserves" and reinsurance cost as items an insurer must add on top of the pure risk cost when pricing compulsory motor. [P] — [MBI report for 2021, s.1.6](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2021.pdf)

**Process, criteria and timing**
- Libra: company founded December 2017; licence granted 6–9 May 2018 for compulsory motor, motor property (own damage + third party) and comprehensive home/business; regulator's operating approval/launch October 2018. First new general-insurance licence since Shlomo Insurance in 2008. [P] — [Calcalist, 9.5.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3737718,00.html); [Calcalist, 23.5.2021](https://www.calcalist.co.il/market/article/BJgxOq8Yd); [Bizportal, 30.6.2021](https://www.bizportal.co.il/capitalmarket/news/article/793945)
- WeSure: licence announced 25 June 2018; commercial activity started 2019. [P] — [Calcalist, 25.6.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3740980,00.html); [Bizportal, 1.4.2021](https://www.bizportal.co.il/general/news/article/790932)
- Anchor (אנקור) and Securitas (סקוריטס): licences granted 5 August 2024 "at the end of a process that took about two years", after "a long examination of their business plans, financial strength and especially IT, operational and regulatory readiness regarding corporate governance". [P] — [Calcalist, 5.8.2024](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/rkcja7ct0)
- Fit-and-proper: the Authority examines the integrity and financial robustness of control-permit applicants and the sources of capital of the applicant and its controllers; a 2013 joint Bank of Israel/CMISA principles paper says control-group members will be required to commit to inject capital into the supervised entity on conditions set by the supervisor. [S] — [BoI/CMISA principles 2013](https://boi.org.il/media/wuvbcctt/201328.pdf); [Commissioner's control-permit policy, via Insurance Association copy](https://www.insurance.org.il/GenericHendler/GetFile.ashx?DG=3328)
- Illustrations of the control test in practice: (i) the heirs of Ayalon's founder "did not receive a control permit and therefore sold control"; the Authority demanded that Ayalon strengthen its equity as a condition for WeSure's permit [P] — [Calcalist, 10.3.2022](https://www.calcalist.co.il/market/article/hjtkwoubc); (ii) a holder below 5% needs no holding permit (DIC's 3.72% stake in Libra) while the same regulator refused Eduardo Elsztain a control permit for Clal [P] — [Calcalist, 10.5.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3737776,00.html).
- 2025 licensing activity: the Commissioner granted three insurers licences for additional lines, granted or updated five control/holding permits, granted "Global Underwriters International Insurance Agency 2025 Ltd" a licence to act as an authorised agent of Lloyd's underwriters (מורשה של חתמי לוידס), approved 397 of 450 officer-approval requests, and issued 131 insurance-agency licences. [P] — [Commissioner's Report 2025, ch.2](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%202_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf)

### Inferences
- [I] WeSure's disclosed surplus (NIS 12.6m at a 136% ratio) implies required capital of roughly NIS 35m and equity of ~NIS 48m against NIS 238m gross / NIS 67.6m retained premium in its second year — i.e. roughly 15% of gross written premium or ~50% of retained premium under the old accounting formula. This is back-solved from two published numbers, not a reported figure.
- [I] Libra's reported equity was ~20% of gross written premium both early (NIS 35.6m on NIS 178.6m in 2020) and late (NIS 183.8m on NIS 917.7m in 2025). That is an observed ratio for one company, not a regulatory requirement.
- [I] Because the SCR phase-in ended with Dec-2024 data and the 2032 run-off applies only to legacy long-term reserves, a 2026–27 entrant gets no transitional capital relief.
- [I] Four entry routes are visible in the record: (1) greenfield insurer (Libra, WeSure, Anchor); (2) agency/Lloyd's coverholder first, insurer licence later (Securitas wrote truck business as a Lloyd's-authorised agency and through Clal before getting its own licence); (3) Lloyd's coverholder licence (granted to a new agency in 2025); (4) buying an existing insurer (WeSure–Ayalon, Harel–Shirbit portfolio). The statute also has a foreign-insurer licence, but I found no foreign motor insurer using it recently.

## 2. Compulsory motor (ביטוח חובה) specifics: Pool, tariffs, levies, long tail, discounting

### Takeaway
Compulsory motor is a regulated, loss-prone line: the regulator's own actuary projected an industry loss ratio above 100% on pure risk alone (113% on 2021 rates, 103% on 2024 rates) before expenses, capital and reinsurance; about a quarter of the net premium goes straight to statutory transfers; claims have a mean duration of 4.6 years; and every compulsory insurer must co-own the Pool and fund its deficit (mostly motorcycles), plus pay 75% of a motorcyclist's injury claim when its insured car is involved. Motorcycles are essentially uninsurable in the free market at current rules — 112 vehicle-years were written outside the Pool in 2024.

### Cited Findings
**Market size and price level**
- Gross premiums 2025: motor property NIS 14,052m (2024: 13,906m; 2023: 12,150m); compulsory motor NIS 6,741m (2024: 6,576m; 2023: 5,828m); all general insurance NIS 35,426m; 19 companies active in general insurance in 2025, two of them new. [P] — [Commissioner's Report 2025, ch.5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf)
- Average compulsory premium for private/commercial vehicles up to 3.5t: NIS 1,595 (2023), 1,683 (2024), 1,756 (2025, +4.3%). [P, chart values] — [same, ch.5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf)
- MBI, underwriting year 2024, free market (excl. Pool): 3,538,562 private-car exposure-years; average net premium NIS 1,155; projected pure risk cost NIS 1,235 excluding the National Insurance (NII) component (1,392 including), i.e. loss ratio 99% before and **107% after loading Pool losses**; taxis 125%; buses 100%; trucks 80%; whole line 103%. Total compulsory exposure 4,128,124 vehicle-years, average net premium NIS 1,395. [P] — [MBI report for 2024, Sept 2025](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- Same exercise on 2021 rates: whole-line projected loss ratio 113% (private cars 110%, 118% after Pool loading); after 2022–23 tariff increases (2023 tariffs ~12% above 2021 nominal, ~4% real) ~109%. [P] — [MBI report for 2021, Dec 2023](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2021.pdf)
- Pure risk cost by definition excludes indirect claims-handling costs, marketing/commissions, G&A, cost of capital and reinsurance. [P] — [MBI 2021, s.1.6](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2021.pdf)

**Tariff filing and permitted rating variables**
- The line was opened to "controlled competition" by the Motor Vehicle Insurance (Insurance under Controlled Competition…) Law 1997, which abolished the uniform tariff; s.14A of that law empowers the Commissioner to set the list of variables and categories insurers may use. [P] — [MBI 2021](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2021.pdf)
- The Authority approves the maximum tariff each insurer may charge; in compulsory motor it has at times refused to approve increases; raising compulsory premiums "depends on the Authority's approval". [P] — [Calcalist, 8.12.2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/b13mcsqz11l); [Calcalist, 21.8.2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/market/article/s13awvntle)
- Insurers may use only variables from the approved risk-classification plan (Appendix 6.2.1 of the consolidated circular); a draft amendment replaces "approval" of tariff formulas with "notification". [S, draft status unknown] — [draft circular text surfaced via search](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1tSiEWSYkq3x5cAMcb8QYUfhApRl21glD/export?format=pdf)
- Mileage (נסועה): Government Decision 1684 (26.6.2022) recorded the Commissioner's intention to add annual kilometres to the permitted list within 180 days; a draft circular was published 1.6.2023; as of the Sept 2025 MBI report "completion is subject to approval by a joint committee of the Knesset Constitution and Finance committees". MBI relativities for privately owned cars (2020–22 claims): ≤5,000 km/yr 0.39 (13% of cars); 5–7k 0.56; 7–9k 0.68; 9–11k 0.75; 11–13k 0.85; 13–16k 0.92; 16–19k 1.00 (base); 19–24k 1.06; 24–32k 1.11; >32k 1.18. [P] — [MBI 2024, ch.4](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- Driver-characteristic data in the MBI (age, sex, seniority etc.) come only from insurers; vehicle data are cross-checked with the Ministry of Transport. [P] — [MBI 2021, s.17](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2021.pdf)
- Address/region: CMISA final position (3.11.2026; draft 22.1.2025) — an insurer may not use area of residence for underwriting or pricing in motor "unless it has proven by statistical studies that the insurance risk differs by geographic area", shown in its notice to the Authority; no insurer had filed such a notice; demanding a clean claims record beyond three years is a prohibited practice. [P] — [Calcalist, 3.11.2026](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/bklpubpafl); [Commissioner's Report 2025, ch.2](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%202_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf)

**Statutory transfers out of each compulsory premium**
- "Tariff additions required by regulations: Karnit 1% of net premium; medical expenses 12.66% of net premium + fees; National Insurance Institute 10% of net premium + fees in 2023–2024 and 10.95% from 2025." [P] — [MBI 2021, s.1.6.16](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2021.pdf)
- NII arrangement: s.328A of the National Insurance Law (amendment 204, in the 2018 Economic Efficiency Law) replaced NII subrogation claims with a fixed up-front share of premiums from 2023; NII payments used to be made about 7 years after the accident; in present-value terms (3% discount) insurers give up ~10–10.95% of premium and shed ~11.0% of claims; NII component estimated at 12.69% of pure risk cost overall (11.24% private cars, 14.37% motorcycles, 19.59% trucks). [P] — [MBI 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- Karnit (קרנית, the fund for victims of uninsured/hit-and-run accidents) is shown in CMISA tables as a compulsory-line entity with NIS 71m premiums in 2025 (1.1% of the compulsory line); accumulated Karnit surpluses were refunded to policyholders via discounts in 2018–2019. [P] — [Commissioner's Report 2025, ch.5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf)

**The Pool (הפול — residual market) and motorcycles**
- The Pool is run by a company whose shareholders are all insurers active in compulsory motor; each insurer's share of results is set by its share of compulsory premiums in a defined period (H1-2025 provisional shares: Phoenix ~12.55%, Harel ~12.01%, Clal ~11.86%, IDI ~10.54%). [S] — [Pool interim report 30.6.2025](https://pool.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/2025-06-30-דוח-כספי-הפול.pdf); [Pool 2025 periodic report](https://pool.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/דוח-תקופתי-2025-סופי.pdf)
- Pool tariff rule (reg. 5(1) of the residual-insurance regulations): basic tariff = free-market pure risk cost for a similar risk group plus at least 25%, provided the expected "loading component" (מרכיב ההעמסה — Pool losses passed to free-market policyholders) does not exceed 8% of the line's pure risk cost; for motorcycles s.7A(b) of the Ordinance sets the expected loading between 5.5% and 8% (8.5% for two years from 1.7.2018). [P] — [MBI 2024, ch.11](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- "Halving order" (צו חצייה "קל-כבד"; amendment 20 to the Road Accident Victims Compensation Law, in force 1.6.2008): when a motorcycle and another vehicle are involved, the other vehicle's insurer pays 75% of the rider's bodily-injury compensation (50% before). About 36% of motorcycle accidents involve another vehicle. [P] — [Commissioner's Report 2025, ch.5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf); [MBI 2024, ch.3](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- Pool, underwriting year 2024: exposure 191,496 vehicle-years (92,190 motorcycles; 91,818 private cars); average premium NIS 2,800; projected loss ratio 165% overall, motorcycles 184% after the halving order (projected 199% for 2025), private cars in the Pool 128%. Motorcycle average net premium ~NIS 3,795 vs pure risk cost NIS 6,978 ex-NII (8,149 incl.); "true" cost before the other vehicle's 75% share ~NIS 11,000, so the premium is about one-third of the real claims cost. Free-market motorcycle exposure: 112 vehicle-years in 2024 (1,985 in 2021). [P] — [MBI 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf); [MBI 2021](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2021.pdf)
- Pool tariff moves: 1.7.2023 private cars +22% on average, motorcycles +10%, some special vehicles +20%; 1.1.2025 motorcycles −8% on average. **Long tail, severity and discounting**
- Mean duration of compulsory claims payments: 4.6 years (NII reimbursements ~7 years); the line has a long "claims tail" and a material IBNR load for recent years. [P] — [MBI 2024, ch.5; MBI 2021, s.17](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- [I] Adding the three statutory items (1% + 12.66% + 10.95%) gives roughly 24–25% of the net premium (bases differ slightly: "net premium" vs "net premium + fees") leaving the insurer before any claims; with the regulator's actuary projecting pure claims at ~100–107% of net premium for private cars, the line cannot make an underwriting profit at industry-average risk selection — profit has to come from better-than-average selection, investment income on 4–5 years of float, or prior-year releases.
- [I] Capital tied up per shekel of compulsory premium is much higher than for motor property because reserves accumulate over ~4.6 years: under the *old* accounting formula the claims basis alone was 20% of retained outstanding claims, and steady-state outstanding claims in a 4.6-year-duration line are several times annual claims. I could not source the Solvency II reserve-risk factor, so no shekel figure is given.
- [I] A "cheaper motorcycle compulsory insurance" proposition would have to beat a Pool price that already sits at about half of the after-halving pure risk cost and one-third of the true cost; the only free-market motorcycle business that can exist is riders whose expected cost is far below the Pool average.
- [I] A new compulsory insurer automatically takes a slice of the Pool deficit proportional to its compulsory market share and pays out under the 75% rule whenever its insured cars hit motorcyclists — both costs are already embedded in the MBI "after Pool loading" numbers.

### Gaps
- The exact Pool loss-sharing formula and the 2025 full-year Pool result by insurer (pool.org.il not reachable; only search excerpts).
- The "9.4% to health funds" figure in the brief was not found; the MBI states 12.66% for medical expenses. Whether 9.4% is an older rate or a different base is unresolved.
- Current list of permitted compulsory rating variables (Appendix 6.2.1) and whether the mileage variable was finally approved after Sept 2025.
- Reserve discount-rate rules under IFRS 17 for Israeli insurers (risk-free curve + illiquidity premium is what MBI uses for its tests; I did not verify the accounting circular) and any "Winograd" 3% capitalisation rule for annuity claims.

## 3. Track record of entrants and smaller players (2008–2026)

### Takeaway
The two 2018 digital entrants ended up in very different places: Libra grew organically to NIS 918m premiums, ~4.2% of both motor lines and an NIS 85m profit in 2025 — but only after five lean years (cumulative 2019–2023 profit about one-fifth of 2024's) and with ~75–80% of premium ceded at the start; WeSure used its listed shell to buy the 14-times-larger Ayalon in 2022 and has since shrunk its own motor book. The 2024 entrants (Anchor, Securitas) are at 0.4–0.9% shares, and Anchor's first-year motor property loss ratio was 105%.

## 4. Did new entrants lower prices? Price gaps, incumbent response, adverse selection

### Takeaway
Structurally the market looks competitive (compulsory HHI fell from 0.29 in 2000 to 0.08; motor property 0.09) and like-for-like quotes differ by only ~15–20%, but the arrival of Libra and WeSure did not prevent motor property prices rising by more than 40% in two years (2022–2024) and record insurer profits; the price fall since late 2024 was driven by falling thefts and an unprecedented regulatory order, not by entrants. Direct and digital writers do run higher loss ratios than agent-based incumbents (67–72% vs ~58–64% in 2025), consistent with giving customers more of the premium back, while saving 10–14 points of commission.

### Cited Findings
- Concentration: compulsory motor HHI 0.29 (2000) → 0.08 (2025), CR3 0.49 → 0.37, CR5 0.60 → 0.54; motor property HHI 0.16 → 0.09, CR3 0.35 → 0.40, CR5 0.49 → 0.57. The Commissioner calls motor "one of the most competitive markets in the economy, certainly in finance". [P] — [Commissioner's Report 2025, ch.5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf); [Calcalist, 8.12.2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/b13mcsqz11l)

### Inferences
- [I] The 3–8 point loss-ratio gap between direct/digital writers (Libra 67%, IDI 68%, WeSure 72%) and agent-based leaders (58–64%) is roughly half of the ~11–14 points those leaders pay in commission — i.e. direct writers appear to share part, not all, of the saved commission with customers through lower premiums (or accept worse risks). The CMISA table cannot separate price from risk mix.
- [I] In a market where like-for-like quotes sit within ~15–20% and group tenders already deliver 15–25% off, a newcomer promising materially lower premiums is competing against the bottom of an already tight distribution; the customers most responsive to a low quote are those the incumbents priced high for a reason — the classic adverse-selection exposure — and with no own claims history the newcomer cannot tell under-priced from over-priced segments.
- [I] The record supports "entrants compress service/switching frictions and take 4–5% share" more than "entrants lower the market price".

## 5. Reinsurance: cession levels, panels, capital relief

### Takeaway
Both 2018 entrants started by ceding roughly three-quarters of their premium (retaining ~20–28%), which is what let them write NIS 180–240m of premium on NIS 35–50m of equity; Libra has since cut its motor property cession. Reinsurer appetite for Israeli motor is narrowing — European reinsurers lost money on motor in 2022–23 and are pulling back, compulsory motor is described as a line reinsurers "have exited", and the small insurers now rely on Africa Re, GIC Re and Korean Re alongside Munich Re.

### Cited Findings
- Libra: retained premiums NIS 17m of NIS 85.6m gross in 2019 and NIS 44m of NIS 178.6m gross in 2020. [P] — [Calcalist, 23.5.2021](https://www.calcalist.co.il/market/article/BJgxOq8Yd)
- WeSure: retained premiums NIS 22.8m of ~100.4m gross in 2019 and NIS 67.6m of ~238m gross in 2020. [P] — [ICE, 1.4.2021](https://www.ice.co.il/finance/news/article/815608)
- Libra's retained earned premium jumped 78.6% in 2024 (gross earned +34.9%) "mainly from reducing the reinsurer's share in motor property in 2024 versus 2023". [P] — [Bizportal, 24.3.2025](https://www.bizportal.co.il/capitalmarket/news/article/20014991)
- Panels (2025): Libra — GIC Re (India) 12.5% of its reinsurance (up from 10%), Africa Re 11%, Korean Re 10%; WeSure — Munich Re almost 60%, GIC 21%, Africa Re 19%. Structures used: quota share plus excess-of-loss. "Quality reinsurance agreements allow companies to reduce their capital requirements" under the solvency reform; Israeli insurers buy reinsurance at higher rates than similar-sized insurers elsewhere; small insurers must use many reinsurers. A senior industry source: "2022 and 2023 were hard years for reinsurers, with losses of tens of millions in motor… some decided to reduce activity in Israel or even exit certain lines… political pressure on European reinsurers to reduce Israeli activity also plays a part". Asian reinsurers are said to pay more slowly and demand more proof. [P] — [Calcalist, 23.7.2026](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/s1f67trvgx)
- Swiss Re — "the largest reinsurer operating in Israel by a large margin" — is running off its Israeli health/disability business after leaving pensions; an industry official: "Reinsurers have exited many areas in Israel that involve risks, such as compulsory car insurance". [P] — [Globes, 31.12.2025](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001530813)
- Compulsory motor: insurers buy XOL for large claims; the MBI now publishes the NII share within large claims specifically for insurers and reinsurers pricing XOL. [P] — [MBI 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- From 2025 CMISA's published loss ratios no longer reflect reinsurance (accounting change); reinsurance arrangements are listed by the regulator among the main drivers of each insurer's solvency ratio. [P] — [Commissioner's Report 2025, ch.3, ch.5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%203_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf)

### Inferences
- [I] Implied premium cession: Libra ~80% (2019) and ~75% (2020); WeSure ~77% (2019) and ~72% (2020). These are 1 − retained/gross from the figures above, blending quota share and XOL cost, not the contractual quota-share percentage.
- [I] Under the old accounting formula the premium basis charged 10%/5% on gross but only 7.5% on retained premium, and the claims basis used retained reserves — so a 75% quota share cut the requirement sharply; under Solvency II the relief depends on reinsurer credit quality (counterparty charge), which matters when the panel shifts toward lower-rated or less familiar reinsurers.
- [I] A 2026–27 entrant should not assume the 2018-vintage terms (75%+ proportional cover on compulsory motor from top European names) are still available.

### Gaps
- Contractual quota-share percentages, ceding commissions and loss-corridor terms for Libra, WeSure, Anchor, Securitas; reinsurers' ratings requirements in the Israeli SCR; current retention ratios (company reports not reachable).
- Whether any reinsurer offers fronting/"capital-light" structures for an Israeli start-up; no example found beyond the Lloyd's coverholder licences.

## 6. Distribution economics: commissions, acquisition cost, importers/leasing, cards and banks

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- [I] An agency/MGA entrant pricing on another insurer's licence would earn somewhere inside the ~10–14% commission band unless it negotiates profit commission; the Wobi outcome (NIS 150m exit with accumulated losses after ~15 years) shows the pure comparison/digital-agency model struggles to recover customer-acquisition cost on annual, low-loyalty motor policies.
- [I] The direct model's saving is real but smaller than the headline commission: Libra's ~13% all-in expense ratio vs incumbents' ~11–14% commission *plus* their own G&A.

## 7. Use of foreign statistics and locally available data for a newcomer

### Takeaway
I found no rule that either permits or forbids pricing on foreign loss data; what the regulator requires is that tariffs and rating variables be supported by statistical/actuarial analysis it can inspect, and its recent positions (address variable; forced refiling of motor property tariffs) show it looks at Israeli claims experience. A newcomer has unusually good *public* data for compulsory motor (the MBI's annual pure-risk tariffs by vehicle and driver variables) and much weaker data for motor property, where incumbents' own experience is the asset.

### Cited Findings
- The MBI was created under s.11A of the Motor Vehicle Insurance Ordinance and the 2004 database regulations to collect premium and claims data from all insurers, analyse it and publish an annual pure-risk report "by vehicle and driver characteristics" plus recommended Pool tariffs; operators: ISO until 2009, Ron-Bina Actuarial 2010–2020, Menavra from 2021 (by tender). The reports include differential tariffs by variable for private cars, motorcycles, taxis, buses, trucks, special vehicles, and supporting Excel tables; the 2024 report adds a first analysis of electric vehicles (~54,000 EVs, heavy IBNR caveat) and mileage relativities. [P] — [MBI 2021](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2021.pdf); [MBI 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- By law compulsory tariffs must rely on variables approved by the supervisor and on statistical analysis. [S] — [search excerpt, candidate URL](https://www.funder.co.il/article/24817)
- CMISA uses the MBI report as an aid in approving insurers' compulsory tariffs and in setting Pool tariffs; a 2024 cross-sector audit of 15 insurers examined statistical reporting. [P] — [Commissioner's Report 2025, ch.2](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%202_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf)
- For the address variable the insurer must prove a risk difference "by statistical studies" reflected in its notice to the Authority when introducing an insurance plan; "it is not clear at this stage which statistical studies will be considered sufficient, whether insurers can rely on their own claims data only". [P] — [Calcalist, 3.11.2026](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/bklpubpafl)
- Individual claims history: "Har HaBituach" (הר הביטוח), the Authority's site, has provided since 18.2.2020 a free consolidated three-year claims-history ("no-claims") report to any vehicle owner; insurers may not demand more than three years. [P] — [Calcalist, 18.2.2020](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3794410,00.html); [Calcalist, 3.11.2026](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/bklpubpafl)
- A separate industry clearing-centre database of property-damage claims on insured vehicles (up to seven years back, paid access) was reported in 2011. [S, dated] — [auto.co.il](https://www.auto.co.il/article/129901-local-news)
- Start-up practice: Libra said it built its own system for "personalised actuarial models" and AI models for churn, purchase propensity and underwriting. [P] — [Calcalist, 23.5.2021](https://www.calcalist.co.il/market/article/BJgxOq8Yd)
- Motor property tariffs: CMISA demanded multi-year pricing data from all insurers and judged tariffs against "risk cost and actual claims". [P] — [Calcalist, 8.12.2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/b13mcsqz11l)

### Inferences
- [I] Foreign statistics could plausibly inform relativities the Israeli data cannot yet support (new EV models, ADAS effects, telematics scores — the MBI itself cites Norwegian and US studies for EVs), but the *level* of Israeli costs is set by local law (no-fault compensation, wage-linked heads of damage, NII and health-fund transfers, the 75% motorcycle rule, theft patterns), so foreign loss costs are not transferable and the regulator's file review is on local experience.
- [I] The realistic sources for a newcomer's first tariff are: MBI tables (compulsory), reinsurers' Israeli portfolio data and pricing support (as Libra and WeSure presumably used, given ~75% cessions), hired actuaries with incumbent experience, and the three-year individual claims history at quote time.

### Gaps
- No explicit CMISA circular or position on the use of non-Israeli loss data was found.
- "Insurance Clearing House (מסלקה ביטוחית)" as a motor claims-history utility: not confirmed — the pension clearing house is a different system; the current operator and content of any shared motor *property* claims database were not found.
- Whether the Pool or the MBI gives licensed insurers record-level (rather than tabulated) data.

## 8. Regulator and government initiatives 2024–2026 affecting competition in motor insurance

### Takeaway
The 2024–2026 agenda has been price intervention and conduct rules rather than structural market opening: two new insurer licences (Aug 2024), an unprecedented order to eight insurers to cut motor property tariffs (Nov 2025–Apr 2026), anti-discrimination and claims-handling positions, and small Pool tariff adjustments. I found no reform of the no-fault law, no restructuring of the Pool, and no invitation to foreign insurers; the garage-pricing reform that was meant to lower claims cost was blocked politically in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- New licences: Anchor and Securitas (5.8.2024); the Finance Minister and Commissioner framed them as increasing competition; 14 insurers operated before them. [P] — [Calcalist, 5.8.2024](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/rkcja7ct0). CMISA attributes stable-to-lower concentration to "growth of small-share companies and the entry of new insurers", while noting the two newcomers' first-year volumes were too small to move the indices. [P] — [Commissioner's Report 2025, ch.5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf)
- Motor property tariff review and forced refiling (details in section 4). [P] — [Commissioner's Report 2025, ch.2](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%202_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf); [Calcalist, 8.12.2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/b13mcsqz11l)

### Inferences
- [I] The regulator's revealed preference is to treat motor as competitive and intervene on price directly when profits look excessive; for an entrant that means the upside from a hard market can be capped by regulatory order (as in 2025–26), while compulsory price increases need approval that is sometimes withheld — an asymmetric payoff.
- [I] WeSure, Libra and the two 2024 entrants were not among the eight insurers ordered to cut tariffs, so small/new writers currently enjoy slightly more pricing latitude than the top eight.

### Gaps
- Any 2024–2026 State Comptroller or Knesset committee recommendations specifically on entry barriers in motor insurance (sites not reachable); the State Comptroller's 2024 cost-of-living chapter on the capital market surfaced in search but was not opened.
- Whether CMISA has pending licence applications from further motor entrants or foreign insurers; nothing found.
- No evidence found of any proposal to change the no-fault Road Accident Victims Compensation Law (חוק הפלת"ד) or the Pool's structure in 2024–2026 beyond tariff levels.
