## 1. Fleet: how many motorcycles and scooters, by engine class; e-scooters/e-bikes; delivery riders; growth 2019–2026

### Takeaway
Israel has roughly 170–185 thousand registered motorcycles and scooters (about 4% of a 4.3-million-vehicle fleet), growing by a record 22.6 thousand new registrations in 2025, and the mix has shifted sharply from ≤125cc scooters to mid-size and heavy machines (bikes above 400cc were about one-third of the fleet in 2024, versus about 15% in 2014). On top of that sit about 201 thousand registered e-bikes and e-kick-scooters that are outside compulsory motor insurance altogether.

[P] — [CBS, Motor Vehicles 2021, Table 32](https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/publications/doclib/2022/1872_motor_vehicles/t32.pdf)

### Inferences
- [I] Putting the series together, the registered motorcycle fleet grew from about 118 thousand (2014, sum of RSA chart labels) to about 156 thousand (2021, CBS) and about 170–178 thousand (2024), i.e. roughly +4% a year; a figure of 185 thousand for 2025/26 is plausible given 22.6 thousand new registrations in 2025 but is trade-press only.
- [I] Motorcycles are about 4% of the vehicle fleet (≈175 thousand of ≈4.3 million) and about 2% of vehicle-km (≈1.3 billion km out of ≈67–69 billion).
- [I] Insured exposure is far smaller than the fleet: 92,302 insured vehicle-years in 2024 against 168–178 thousand registered motorcycles (see section 2 for the 45% uninsured rate).

### Gaps
- I could not open the CBS "Motor Vehicles" publications for 2023–2025, so there is no official engine-class table after 2021; the 2024 split comes from chart labels in the RSA report and my class assignment.
- No official count of delivery riders (Wolt and similar) or of scooters used for deliveries was found; company-owned insured exposure (17,147) is only a proxy and excludes couriers riding on private-use policies or uninsured.
- No reliable 2026 fleet total from an official source.

## 2. The Pool (הפול): how it works, ownership, share of motorcycle policies, tariffs, premiums, claims, deficits, and how losses are shared

### Takeaway
The Pool is a residual-market insurer jointly owned and funded by the commercial insurers, with tariffs set by the Capital Market Authority below cost; it writes essentially all motorcycle compulsory insurance (92,190 of 92,302 insured vehicle-years in 2024; only 112 in the free market). Riders pay an average net premium of about NIS 3,800 against an expected claims cost of about NIS 7,000–8,150 after the 75% recovery from other vehicles' insurers (about NIS 11,000 before it), so premiums fund only about 40–44% of claims, with the rest coming from the splitting order (about 31%) and a deficit spread across all other insured motorists (about 25%).

### Cited Findings
**What it is and who owns it**
- The Pool is "a corporation owned by a number of insurance companies" that insures in compulsory insurance vehicles and drivers who, because of excess risk, were not insured by other insurers; its tariff is set by the Capital Market, Insurance and Savings Authority; plaintiffs' lawyers describe it as having "exclusive control of the motorcycle insurance market". [P] — [Calcalist, 30.10.2024](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/rkbzmijw1e)
- It is "in effect an insurance company whose costs are shared by all private insurers according to their market share of total compulsory-insurance premiums". [P] — [Globes, 16.3.2023](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001441430). The statistical operator confirms: the Pool's loss is divided according to the total premium actually collected by each company, while the loading on tariffs is applied to pure risk cost. [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, §1.5.3](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- Legal name of the managing entity: התאגיד המנהל של המאגר לביטוחי רכב חובה ("הפול") בע"מ; it publishes annual and half-year financial statements and monthly tariff sheets. [S] — [Pool periodic report 2024](https://pool.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/דוח-תקופתי-הפול-2024.pdf)
- A State Comptroller follow-up report on residual motor insurance noted that the Commissioner's authority to approve tariffs containing a loading was not explicitly anchored in legislation, and earlier found that only about 50% of riders hold valid compulsory insurance. [S] — [State Comptroller, "הסדרי ביטוח שיורי לכלי רכב — ממצאי מעקב"](https://library.mevaker.gov.il/sites/DigitalLibrary/Pages/Reports/465-9.aspx)

**Share of motorcycle compulsory policies**
- 2024 motorcycle exposure: 92,302 insured vehicle-years in total, of which 92,190 in the Pool and 112 in the free market; the operator bases motorcycle pricing on Pool experience only "because in recent years free-market motorcycle exposure is negligible (אפסית)". [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, Tables 1001A–C and §8.2.1](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- Motorcycles were about 49% of the Pool's insureds in 2024 (51% in 2023), in exposure terms. Motorcycles insured in the Pool: 128,437 (2022), 127,800 (2023), 130,512 (2024, +2.12%). [P] — [Commissioner's report for 2024, ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/report_0010/he/Commisioner%20Report%20Design%20B_chapter5_%20ver%206.0_digital.pdf); [Commissioner's report for 2023, ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/report_0006/he/The_Commissioner's_Report_Chapter_5.pdf.pdf). **Conflict:** the operator's 92,190 vehicle-years versus the Commissioner's 130,512 "insured motorcycles" (see Inferences).
- 2021: about 290 thousand vehicles insured in the Pool, of which about 125 thousand motorcycles (43%) responsible for about 90% of its losses. [P] — [Calcalist, 21.3.2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/hyvk00ddgh). Alongside motorcycles the Pool insured about 35 thousand private cars of refused drivers plus heavy engineering vehicles. [P] — [Globes, 16.3.2023](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001441430). In 2024 Pool exposure was 191,496 vehicle-years: 92,190 motorcycles, 91,818 private/light commercial, 6,287 special vehicles. [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, Table 1001B](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)

**Tariff structure and actual prices**
- Average net premium actually collected per insured motorcycle-year (CPI-linked to 12/2024): 2017: 3,873; 2018: 3,898; 2019: 3,880; 2020: 3,928; 2021: 3,912; 2022: 3,891; 2023: 3,596; 2024: 3,795 NIS. [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, Table 303](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- Average 2024 premium by sub-group (NIS, underwriting year 2024): privately owned without deductible 4,134 (25,607 exposure-years); privately owned with deductible 2,886 (49,548); all privately owned 3,311 (75,155); company-owned without deductible 6,539 (13,538); company-owned with deductible 3,575 (3,609); all company-owned 5,915 (17,147). [P] — [same report, Table 30](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- **Pool tariff sheet, October 2026 (private ownership, "gross", NIS a year — read only as a search excerpt of a garbled PDF, low confidence):** ≤50cc ≈ 3,419; 51–125cc ≈ 5,181; 126–250cc ≈ 5,230; 251–345cc ≈ 7,458; 346–590cc ≈ 7,922; above 590cc ≈ 8,065; non-private ownership is higher, top tier ≈ 10,575. Multipliers: motorcycle used by any rider ×1.45; learner motorcycle ×1.25; rented motorcycle ×2; no surcharge for a sidecar; a policy with a deductible of NIS 25,000 on non-pecuniary damage plus 7 days of lost earnings gets a 30% discount. [S] — [Pool tariff, October 2026](https://pool.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/תעריף-אוקטובר-2026-003.pdf); [Pool tariff, July 2026](https://pool.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/תעריף-יולי-2026.pdf); [Pool tariff, March 2025](https://pool.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/תעריף-מרץ-2025.pdf)
- Price range quoted in the press: compulsory insurance "from about NIS 2,000 up to about NIS 6,000" (2023) [P] — [Bizportal](https://www.bizportal.co.il/car/news/article/813917); average motorcycle premium NIS 3,000–4,000 and up to NIS 6,000 in extreme cases versus an average NIS 1,440 for four-wheel vehicles (2018) [P] — [Calcalist, 29.1.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3730607,00.html); a 20-year-old with an A2 licence on a 125cc paid "more than NIS 4,000" (about 2018) [S] — [Ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/wheels/article/5279576); "average premium close to NIS 4,000" (2021) [P] — [Calcalist op-ed, 19.10.2021](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/by5xdbhsk).
- Business-use classification: answering yes to any of three questions (for example claiming the motorcycle as a tax expense, or using it for work) moves the policy to the "business" tariff and can add up to about NIS 1,500; the Authority's stated rationale is that a business user should not be subsidised at the same rate as a private user. [P] — [Calcalist op-ed, 19.10.2021](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/by5xdbhsk)
- Safety-system discounts exist in the Pool tariff (each system earns a varying discount; information is not always on the registration document). A class action filed in December 2022 alleged riders were not told; in the October 2024 settlement a sample showed 96% had received the discounts, the Pool agreed to highlight the discount and store safety-system data, and refunds were described as "hundreds of shekels" per affected insured. [P] — [Calcalist, 30.10.2024](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/rkbzmijw1e)
- Tariff changes: on 1 July 2023 Pool motorcycle tariffs rose about 10% on average (the first update in more than a decade), private cars in the Pool about 22%, some special vehicles about 20%; in January 2025 the Authority cut Pool motorcycle tariffs by about 8% on average. [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, §1.4.5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf); [Calcalist, 21.3.2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/hyvk00ddgh). **Conflict:** a Funder report puts the cut at "about 7%" on average, and a riders' organisation called it a "false presentation" of a reduction. [S] — [Funder](https://www.funder.co.il/article/171126)
- Monthly tariff sheets suggest small index-type updates through 2026 (for example the smallest private-car band moved from about NIS 3,533 in April to 3,553 in June and 3,584 in August 2026); no structural change to the motorcycle tariff in 2026 was found. [S] — [Pool tariff, August 2026](https://pool.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/תעריף-אוגוסט-2026-002.pdf)

### Inferences
- [I] The gap between 130,512 "motorcycles insured in the Pool" (Commissioner, from Pool financial statements) and 92,190 exposure vehicle-years (operator) is most likely definitional: many riders buy cover for only part of the year (the Motorcycle Club's lawyer said in 2018 that riders "insure only for the period of the annual test"), so the count of insured machines exceeds vehicle-years. This is my reading; neither source reconciles the two.
- [I] The search-excerpt tariff figures for October 2026 (NIS 5,181 for 51–125cc, NIS 8,065 above 590cc) are far above the 2024 average net premium of NIS 3,795 and the NIS 2,886 average paid by private riders with a deductible. They are therefore probably base or top-of-range gross rates before the 30% deductible discount and age/seniority/safety discounts, not typical prices. I could not verify the age and seniority factors of the Pool tariff.
- [I] Total cross-subsidy to insured riders in 2024 ≈ NIS 467 million (208 deficit + 259 splitting order) ≈ 56% of motorcycle claims cost ≈ NIS 3,600 per insured motorcycle (130,512 basis) or ≈ NIS 5,100 per insured vehicle-year (92,190 basis).

### Gaps
- The Pool's own tariff PDFs and financial statements (pool.org.il) could not be opened, so actual NIS prices by rider age and licence seniority for 2024–2026 are missing; only base figures by engine band from a garbled search excerpt are available.
- The list of shareholder insurers and their percentages in the Pool was not obtained (the half-year 2024 statements reportedly list insurer shares for underwriting years 2022–2024).
- 2025 full-year Pool results (claims, premium, deficit) and the Commissioner's 2025 chapter were not read.

## 3. Cross-subsidy: the loading on other motorists, its rate and NIS amount, its history, reforms 2023–2026, and private insurers outside the Pool

### Takeaway
Two statutory mechanisms subsidise riders: the "loading component" (Pool deficits spread over all free-market compulsory policies, legally capped within a 5.5%–8% band of the line's pure risk cost) and the splitting order (the car's insurer pays 75% of the rider's injury claim regardless of fault). The motorcycle part of the loading cost other motorists on average NIS 91 per insured vehicle in 2022, NIS 64 in 2023 and NIS 53 in 2024 (NIS 208–336 million a year), and the operator recommends an 8.0% loading for 2026 pricing, about NIS 90 on a private car; no private insurer writes motorcycle compulsory cover at any meaningful scale.

### Cited Findings
- **How the two mechanisms work (official description):** (1) splitting order "light–heavy": where another vehicle is involved, 75% of the motorcycle claim is paid by the insurer of the other vehicle; (2) subsidy of the Pool's loss-making tariffs up to a ceiling of 8% of the line's pure risk cost (the "loading component"). Motorcycle tariffs are below their risk cost, so significant losses arise in the Pool and are imposed on the whole insured public. [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, executive summary and §1.6.5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- **Splitting order origin:** Amendment 20 to the Road Accident Victims Compensation Law, in force 1 June 2008: in an accident between a motorcycle and a non-motorcycle vehicle, the other vehicle's insurer pays the motorcycle's insurer 75% of the rider's bodily-injury compensation, instead of 50% before. [P] — [Commissioner's report for 2024, footnote 11](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/report_0010/he/Commisioner%20Report%20Design%20B_chapter5_%20ver%206.0_digital.pdf). About 36% of motorcycle accidents (claims) involve another vehicle. [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, ch. 3](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf). The transfer applies even when the accident was the rider's fault. [P] — [Globes, 16.3.2023](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001441430)
- **Loading band and current level:** under the regulations Pool tariffs must be set so that the loading component does not exceed 8% of total pure risk cost in compulsory motor insurance; the operator recommends aiming at the middle of the 5.5%–8% range. The computed loading component was 5.9% for underwriting year 2024 and is projected at 6.8% for 2026; expressed as an add-on to free-market pure risk cost it is 6.9% (2024) and 8.0% (recommended for 2026 pricing), down from 8.4% in the previous year's report "following favourable development of motorcycle claims". [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, §§2.3.3, 11.3, 11.4, Tables 64–65](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- **NIS per policy:** free-market private-car pure risk cost (excluding the NII share) is NIS 1,144 before and NIS 1,235 after loading Pool losses, i.e. about NIS 91 per private car; the private-car loss ratio moves from 99% to 107% once Pool losses are loaded. [P] — [same report, Tables 1001C and 1000C](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- **Average subsidy per insured vehicle for motorcycle losses only (Authority's chart, NIS):** 2023: 64; 2024: 53; chart values for 2016–2022 range from 87 to 135, with 91 for 2022 on my reading; computed as the Pool's annual motorcycle loss divided by the number of insured vehicles, excluding splitting-order money. [P] — [Commissioner's report for 2024, chart 11](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/report_0010/he/Commisioner%20Report%20Design%20B_chapter5_%20ver%206.0_digital.pdf); [Commissioner's report for 2023, chart 11](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/report_0006/he/The_Commissioner's_Report_Chapter_5.pdf.pdf)
- **Other published per-driver figures (conflict by definition):** private-car owners "each contributed on average NIS 175" in 2021 to cover about NIS 340 million of Pool-paid damages [P] — [Globes, 16.3.2023](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001441430); the Authority estimated "about NIS 180 on average per insured per year" (July 2023) [P] — [Calcalist, 6.7.2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/investing/article/sjv0wlet2); "about NIS 95" rising to NIS 125 (2018) [P] — [Calcalist, 29.1.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3730607,00.html); an older column put the add-on at about NIS 60 per private-car policy plus about NIS 40 for the splitting order [S] — [Walla, 2012](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/2519860).
- **History of the loading rate:** a 2011 law led by MK Faina Kirshenbaum created the loading component; Calcalist says the original rate was 6% of total insurance cost and rose to 6.5% about six years before 2018 [P] — [Calcalist, 29.1.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3730607,00.html). In 2018 a private bill by MK Miki Zohar sought 11%; the compromise with Finance Minister Kahlon raised the ceiling to 8.5% of pure risk cost for two years and 8% thereafter (bill by MKs Zohar, Cabel and Vaknin, passed second and third readings). [S] — [Federation of Chambers of Commerce summary](https://www.chamber.org.il/sectors/1238/1251/13506/13507/89141/); it took effect in July 2018, most insurers applied it in 2019, and the rate fell to a permanent 8% in mid-2020 [S] — [Walla cars](https://cars.walla.co.il/item/3205975). **Conflict:** Calcalist describes the pre-2018 rate as 6% in one article and 6.5% in another; the Knesset summary says 6.5%.
- **2018 debate numbers:** the Authority's Nir Bartal told the Knesset there were 130 thousand motorcycles of which about 40% rode uninsured; in 2016 NIS 600 million was paid to insured injured riders, of which 250 million came from riders' premiums and 350 million from car owners' loading; the extra burden per private-car owner from the increase was estimated at NIS 40 at most. [P] — [Calcalist, 21.2.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3732436,00.html). Calcalist's earlier breakdown: of about NIS 650 million of riders' accident costs, about 260 million came through the loading, about 200 million through the splitting order, and only about 200 million from riders themselves; the deal avoided an increase of about NIS 1,000 per rider. [P] — [Calcalist, 29.1.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3730607,00.html)
- **2023–2026 changes and proposals:** in March 2023 the Authority said the subsidy had reached the legal limit (5.5%–8%) and raised rider tariffs 10% from July 2023 to reduce it; in January 2025 it cut them about 8%, stating the subsidy stays within the permitted level [P] — [Calcalist, 21.3.2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/hyvk00ddgh); [Globes, 16.3.2023](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001441430); [Statistical operator report 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf). A Knesset Economics Committee discussion on the rise in motorcycle compulsory prices was scheduled in July 2023 [P] — [Calcalist, 6.7.2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/investing/article/sjv0wlet2); a plan to cancel the 2023 increase was reported in June 2023 [S] — [Walla cars](https://cars.walla.co.il/item/3586521). A 2023 Knesset Research Center paper on new vehicle types discusses the two cross-subsidy channels (splitting order and loading) and expects the motorcycle category may enlarge cross-subsidy [S] — [Knesset RIC](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/globaldocs/MMM/60abf40d-2776-ee11-815f-005056aac6c3/2_60abf40d-2776-ee11-815f-005056aac6c3_11_20440.pdf). In November 2025 Commissioner Amit Gal sent an unusual letter to nine large insurers about a roughly 30% jump in motor insurance prices in 2021–2024; the same coverage notes that without the subsidy motorcycle compulsory prices would be far higher [S] — [Kipa](https://www.kipa.co.il/1216513-0).
- **Private insurers outside the Pool:** free-market motorcycle exposure was 112 vehicle-years in 2024, with only 6 expected claims [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, Table 1001C](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf). "Commercial insurers do not provide compulsory insurance for motorcycles" (2009) [P] — [Globes](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1000502519); "most insurers refuse to insure them directly" (2023) [P] — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/hyvk00ddgh); where private insurers do quote, they charge "NIS 5,000 and more" for some motorcycles while the Pool can be half that [P] — [Globes, 16.3.2023](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001441430).

### Inferences
- [I] The different per-driver figures are not contradictory once definitions are separated: NIS 53–91 (Authority chart) is the Pool's motorcycle loss only, divided by all insured vehicles; NIS 91 per private car is the 8% loading on a private car's pure risk cost for 2026 pricing (whole-Pool deficit, including refused car drivers); NIS 175–180 appears to combine the loading with the splitting-order transfers (roughly NIS 340 million + 332 million over about 3.6 million vehicles ≈ NIS 185).
- [I] Adding the splitting order (NIS 259 million in 2024, roughly NIS 65–75 per insured non-motorcycle vehicle) to the motorcycle deficit (NIS 208 million, NIS 53 per vehicle), other motorists paid on the order of NIS 120–130 per vehicle in 2024 for riders' injuries, down from about NIS 180 in 2021–2022.
- [I] The loading is a regulatory ceiling on Pool under-pricing, not a fixed tax: when motorcycle claims or exposure rise, the Authority must raise Pool tariffs (as in July 2023) to stay inside 8%. This makes rider prices sensitive to the number of insured riders: more insured riders at under-priced tariffs means a bigger deficit.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found of any reform in 2026 changing the 5.5%–8% band or the 75% splitting rate; I cannot rule out one, because Knesset and Authority circular pages could not be opened.
- The exact legal text (Motor Vehicle Insurance Ordinance provisions and the regulations setting the band) was not read.
- No named private insurer currently selling motorcycle compulsory cover, or its price list, was identified.

## 4. Claims experience: deaths and serious injuries, share of casualties versus fleet and kilometres, claim frequency and severity under the no-fault law, loss ratio, and the actuarially fair premium

### Takeaway
Motorcyclists are about 4% of the fleet and about 2% of kilometres but 22% of road deaths (98 killed in 2024, 101 in 2025) and about a quarter of all killed-or-seriously-injured; in insurance terms a motorcycle generates a bodily-injury claim 4.5 times as often as a private car (5.8% versus 1.3% a year) at a higher average cost (about NIS 141 thousand versus 101 thousand), for an expected cost about six times a car's. The official actuarially fair pure premium is about NIS 8,150 per motorcycle-year including the NII share (about NIS 7,000 excluding it, about NIS 11,000 before the 75% splitting-order recovery) against an average premium of about NIS 3,800 — a loss ratio of 184% in 2024, projected at 199% after the January 2025 tariff cut.

### Cited Findings
**Road casualties**
- Motorcycle riders killed per year (RSA chart 8.1): 2014: 38; 2015: 56; 2016: 41; 2017: 44; 2018: 62; 2019: 64; 2020: 68; 2021: 85; 2022: 77; 2023: 77; 2024: 98 (+27% on 2023). Killed plus seriously injured: 460 (2014) → 748 (2024), +62.6%, a statistically significant rise; riders were 19%–25% of all killed-and-seriously-injured in each year of 2014–2024. Total road deaths in 2024: 439. [P] — [RSA, trends 2014–2024, ch. 8 and foreword](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/trends_2024/he/research_trends_2024_v03.pdf)

- 2025: 459 road deaths (439 in 2024); 101 motorcyclists killed, about 22% of all deaths, versus 41 riders (about 11%) in 2016; motorcycles' involvement rate in serious accidents is about 20 times that of private cars. [S] — [auto.co.il on the RSA trends bulletin](https://www.auto.co.il/articles/car-news/local-news/road-safety-2025/); [Maariv](https://www.maariv.co.il/news/israel/article-1385951)

- Young riders (age up to 24): 43 killed in 2024 (31 in 2023) and 43 again in 2025 — the decade's record, nearly four times the level a decade earlier; 233 seriously injured in 2024 and 250 in 2025; young riders were 41% of all riders killed in 2024. [P] — [ICE, 5.5.2025](https://www.ice.co.il/auto/news/article/1063182); [ICE, 2.6.2026](https://www.ice.co.il/auto/news/article/1115154)
- Risk per kilometre: in 2024 heavy motorcycles (401cc+) were involved in 816 serious accidents per billion km versus 36 for private cars, about 23 times; involvement per km rose significantly for heavy motorcycles over 2014–2024 while falling for most vehicle types. [P] — [RSA trends 2014–2024, chart 2.3](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/trends_2024/he/research_trends_2024_v03.pdf). [P] — [Bizportal 2023](https://www.bizportal.co.il/car/news/article/813917). Riders are involved in fatal accidents at 11 times their share of kilometres; more than a third of rider accidents are single-vehicle. [P] — [Calcalist, 14.2.2022, quoting the RSA's acting director](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/bkeppdwj5)
- Licensing and age: one third of riders killed or seriously injured (2014–2024) held no licence or an unsuitable one, about half for bikes above 500cc; in 2024, 234 riders aged 15–24 were killed or seriously injured out of 61,117 licence holders of that age = 38.3 per 10,000, more than 6.6 times the mid-age rate; riders with one year of experience are several times more involved than veterans. [P] — [RSA trends 2014–2024, charts 8.3–8.6](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/trends_2024/he/research_trends_2024_v03.pdf)
- Older Pool statement (2009): motorcycles' involvement in serious accidents 5.6 times other vehicles; chance of needing medical rehabilitation 27.5 times that of a closed vehicle. [P] — [Globes, 5.10.2009](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1000502519)

**The no-fault feature**
- Compulsory insurance covers bodily injury to the insured rider, passengers and pedestrians hit, and under Israeli law anyone injured in a motorcycle accident is covered "even if the accident was caused by his own fault". [P, law-firm sponsored article] — [Calcalist, 15.5.2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/article/ryrlti1s2)
- A rider without valid compulsory insurance loses the no-fault route but may sue the at-fault party in tort (where the statutory income ceiling and pain-and-suffering caps of the compensation law do not apply): a district court awarded about NIS 3.5 million (less NIS 785 thousand of NII benefits) to an uninsured rider injured in 2017, the judge noting that "the phenomenon of motorcycle accidents without compulsory insurance is expanding"; in a 2026 district ruling pain and suffering was assessed at NIS 400 thousand and the court refused to reduce compensation because the rider chose not to insure, saying that would need legislation. [P] — [ICE, 28.7.2025](https://www.ice.co.il/law/news/article/1076662); [ICE, 28.10.2026](https://www.ice.co.il/bdispot/news/article/1135504)

### Inferences
- [I] Motorcycles are 2.2% of insured vehicle-years (92,302 of 4,128,124) and about 6.1% of net premium (350 of 5,761 NIS million) but 11.1% of expected compulsory-line claims after the splitting order (752 of 6,762 NIS million); adding back splitting-order transfers (≈ NIS 259–337 million a year), riders account for roughly 15% of the line's bodily-injury cost.
- [I] Fleet share ≈ 4%, kilometre share ≈ 2%, death share ≈ 22% (98/439 in 2024; 101/459 in 2025): riders are over-represented in deaths by a factor of about 5–6 relative to vehicles and about 11 relative to kilometres, consistent with the RSA's own "×11" statement.
- [I] Insured claim frequency understates true casualty incidence because 45% of machines are uninsured and their riders' own injuries fall outside the no-fault scheme (they claim in tort or from NII, and passengers/pedestrians from Karnit).
- [I] Relative to a private car in 2024: frequency ×4.5, average claim ×1.4, pure risk cost ×6.2 (8,149 versus 1,320), while the average premium is only ×3.2 (3,795 versus 1,170).

### Gaps

- Kilometre share is my estimate from chart labels; no official "share of vehicle-km" figure for motorcycles was found.

## 5. Property side: comprehensive, theft and third-party cover for motorcycles — availability, price, theft rates, insurers

### Takeaway
Comprehensive (מקיף) and third-party property (צד ג') cover for motorcycles is voluntary, sold by commercial insurers at freely set prices, and by all accounts bought by a minority of riders because of cost; I found no current, citable NIS price data or official loss statistics for it. Theft exposure is material and rising: tracking companies put motorcycles at 11% (Ituran) to about 20% (Pointer, with an 80% jump) of theft events in 2025, far above their 4% fleet share.

### Cited Findings
- Compulsory insurance has nothing to do with the value of the motorcycle; comprehensive and third-party policies are "much more dynamic" in price because they are not compulsory, competition among insurers is greater, and shopping around can find worthwhile rates; extended cover is advised for expensive bikes and for new, young riders. [P] — [Bizportal, 29.4.2023](https://www.bizportal.co.il/car/news/article/813917); [Globes, 5.10.2009](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1000502519)
- Most riders in Israel did not buy comprehensive cover because of high cost, and some motorcycle policies cover theft of parts or accessories only if the whole motorcycle is stolen (2020). [S] — [Walla finance](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3379890)
- Insurers known to have offered motorcycle property cover: Direct Insurance (ביטוח ישיר) launched comprehensive and third-party cover for motorcycles and scooters in July 1998 with differential pricing by rider age, family status, claims history and bike type, excluding off-road and sport bikes; a 1998 Piaggio 50cc cost NIS 836–894 a year for good riders versus NIS 1,050 at Migdal; there were then about 80,000 two-wheelers. [P, historical only] — [Globes, 6.7.1998](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=82892). Bituach Haklai (ביטוח חקלאי) publishes a motorcycle policy application form [S, title only] — [bth.co.il](https://www.bth.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/בַּקָּשָׁה-לַעֲרִיכַת-פּוֹלִיסָה-לְבִטּוּחַ-אוֹפַנּוֹעַ-302-1-02-02.pdf). The Phoenix has a two-wheeler insurance page dated January 2022 [S, title only] — [fnx.co.il](https://www.fnx.co.il/car-insurance/car_information/insurance-reform-two-wheeled-vehicle/).
- Theft: Ituran's 2025 study — vehicle-theft events fell about 21% versus 2024; by segment of theft attempts: SUVs 47%, private cars 34%, motorcycles 11%, commercial 5%, trucks 2%; 47% of thefts in Gush Dan; 52% of stolen vehicles go to the parts trade. [P] — [ICE, 28.12.2025](https://www.ice.co.il/auto/news/article/1097534). Pointer's 2025 summary — thefts fell for the first time in four years, but "motorcycles are a fifth of thefts, with a drastic increase of 80%", and Q4 2025 theft attempts rose 25% on Q3. [P] — [Globes, 6.1.2026](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001531135). **Conflict:** 11% (Ituran, attempts by segment) versus about 20% (Pointer); these are each company's own subscriber samples, not police data.
- The police have refused to publish full theft counts; data for 2025 were obtained through freedom-of-information requests and focus on cars. [S] — [auto.co.il](https://www.auto.co.il/articles/car-news/industry/police-refuse-car-theft-data/)
- Car-market context for property cover: comprehensive car premiums fell more than 10% in 2025 after rising about 30% in 2024, with third-party nearly unchanged [P] — [Globes, 6.1.2026](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001531135); a Wobi index reported a 13% year-on-year fall in comprehensive prices in Q3 2026 and Bizportal reported comprehensive prices down 25% from the peak with wide dispersion between companies (November 2026) — cars, not motorcycles. [S / P headline] — [Bizportal, 3.11.2026](https://www.bizportal.co.il/Insurance/news/article/20047090)
- A trade-press headline reports that "for the first time, use of advanced safety gear will lead to cheaper insurance policies for two-wheelers" (details and date not seen). [S, headline only] — [queenoftheroad.co.il](https://www.queenoftheroad.co.il/?p=79004)

### Inferences
- [I] Because the property side is unregulated in price and (unlike compulsory cover) not subsidised, it is where private insurers can select and price riders freely; the thin evidence of take-up suggests high prices relative to bike values and strict acceptance rules, but this is not quantified by any source I could read.
- [I] If motorcycles are 11–20% of thefts on about 4% of the fleet, theft frequency per machine is roughly 3–5 times that of the average vehicle, which would make theft cover expensive or conditional on immobilisers/trackers.

### Gaps
- No current price examples (NIS a year) for comprehensive, theft-only or third-party motorcycle policies; no list of insurers currently accepting motorcycles; no acceptance criteria (age, bike type, protection requirements).
- No official statistics on motorcycle property insurance penetration, premium volume or loss ratio (the Commissioner's property-line data are not split by vehicle type in the pages read).
- No police or official count of stolen motorcycles.

## 6. Regulation: pricing freedom, permitted rating variables, telematics / safety-course / gear discounts, and proposals to change the no-fault cover for riders

### Takeaway
A private insurer may price motorcycle compulsory insurance only with rating variables approved by the Commissioner and, in practice, below the Pool's tariff (maximum tariffs are tied to Pool prices, about 90% of them), while the Pool's own tariff is set by the Authority. Mileage and a motorcycle training course were proposed as new variables in a June 2023 draft circular but, per the operator's September 2025 report, still awaited approval by a joint Knesset committee. Proposals to narrow riders' no-fault benefits have recurred since 2007 (a deductible option exists since 2008; a "lean policy" committee was set up in 2018), but I found no enacted change removing two-wheelers from the no-fault law.

### Cited Findings
- **Approved rating variables (as of June 2023):** insurers may set compulsory tariffs only with variables from a list approved by the Commissioner: vehicle ownership, engine volume, airbags, ESP, ABS, FCW, LDW, motorcycle safety systems, and five driver characteristics (age, licence seniority, sex, number of accidents, licence suspensions). [P] — [Bizportal, 3.6.2023](https://www.bizportal.co.il/Insurance/news/article/815125)
- **Proposed new variables (draft circular published 1 June 2023):** mileage (kilometres driven), autonomous emergency braking (AEB), lane-keeping assist (LDP), and a **motorcycle training course** (added in cooperation with the National Road Safety Authority); the final wording is subject to approval by a joint committee of the Knesset Constitution and Finance committees. [P] — [Bizportal, 3.6.2023](https://www.bizportal.co.il/Insurance/news/article/815125)
- **Mileage status:** Government Decision 1684 of 26 June 2022 recorded the Commissioner's intention to add mileage within 180 days; the draft circular followed on 1 June 2023; in September 2025 the operator still writes that the information "will help … when the mileage variable is approved", and that completion is subject to the joint committee's approval. For motorcycles the operator already publishes mileage tables for private and company-owned bikes. [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, ch. 4](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- **How the tariff is legally built:** the operator's recommended risk tariffs are, by law, the basis for the Pool's compulsory tariffs [S] — [Funder](https://www.funder.co.il/article/146457); the Pool tariff is set by the Authority, which "sets tariffs and limits their cost so that the tariff is below the risk cost" to keep riders insured [P] — [Calcalist, 21.3.2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/hyvk00ddgh); insurers' maximum compulsory tariffs "depend on Pool prices and must be lower than them (about 90%)" [P] — [Globes, 16.3.2023](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001441430). The governing statute cited for the variable list is section 14A of the Motor Vehicle Insurance Law (insurance under controlled competition), 1997 [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, ch. 4](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf).
- **Operator's recommended differential model for privately owned motorcycles (GLM on 2020–2022 data; relativities, share of exposure in brackets):** deductible: none 1.00 (38%), with 0.61 (62%). Youngest rider's age: 17–22 1.00 (6%); 23–29 0.66 (16%); 30–32 0.47; 33–36 0.54; 37–40 0.47; 40–45 0.52; 46–51 0.50; 52–59 0.22 (13%); 60+ 0.18 (13%). Licence seniority: up to 1 year 1.00 (10%); 2–3 years 0.86; 4–9 years 0.64; 10+ years 0.61 (64%). Accident history: none 1.00 (95%); one or more 1.56. Engine: ≤125cc 1.00 (29%); 126–250cc 1.45; 251–345cc 2.12; 346–445cc 1.90; 446–590cc 2.03; 591–685cc 2.51; 686–995cc 3.05; above 995cc 3.00. Base constant NIS 14,662 (includes ×1.08 Pool-loss loading and ×0.841 NII reduction). The operator notes these are raw model results and that smoothing should be considered for a final tariff. [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, Table 308](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- **Company-owned motorcycles:** constant NIS 18,166; not-named rider without deductible 1.00 (45%); named rider without deductible 0.59 (41%); named rider with deductible 0.24 (14%). [P] — [same report, Table 408](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- **Horsepower instead of engine volume:** Pool premiums are currently set by engine volume; the operator presents an alternative with power bands (up to 15 hp/125cc 1.00 … above 102 hp 1.92; accident history 1.80) because small engines can now be powerful, and licence classes have followed European power-based rules since 2007. [P] — [same report, Table 312](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- **Deductible option (the one enacted benefit reduction):** a 2008 amendment allowed insurers of two-wheelers to sell compulsory policies with a deductible on non-pecuniary damage (up to NIS 25,000) and loss of earnings (first 7 days) in exchange for a discount. [P, law-firm sponsored article] — [Calcalist, 15.5.2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/article/ryrlti1s2). In 2009 the discount was about 11% and about 95% of riders declined it [P] — [Globes, 5.10.2009](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1000502519); the Pool tariff now gives 30% [S] — [Pool tariff](https://pool.org.il/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/תעריף-מרץ-2025.pdf), and in 2024 about two-thirds of privately owned insured motorcycles (49,548 of 75,155) carried the deductible [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, Table 30](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf).
- **Safety course:** the RSA awarded a tender (February 2022) for a safe-riding pilot for more than 200 riders at NIS 400 (versus NIS 1,000–2,000 for advanced courses); success of the pilot was described as essential to persuading the Commissioner to approve a compulsory-insurance discount for graduates. [P] — [Calcalist, 14.2.2022](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/bkeppdwj5). A three-step plan (larger deductible, discount for safe-riding course graduates, recognition of safety systems) was expected to yield 3–5% each, about 10% together. [S] — [Walla cars](https://cars.walla.co.il/item/3564020)
- **Telematics:** no approved telematics/behaviour variable for compulsory insurance was found; the idea of pricing riders by distance, urban versus inter-urban route and riding style (a cheap chip monitoring braking and acceleration) appears as a lawyer's proposal, which also calls for abolishing the private/business split. [P] — [Calcalist op-ed, 19.10.2021](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/by5xdbhsk)
- **Proposals on the no-fault cover:** the 2018 Zohar–Kahlon compromise included setting up a committee to advance "an adapted and narrower compulsory insurance for motorcyclists" [P] — [Calcalist, 29.1.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3730607,00.html); a joint Finance–Justice committee was to design a "lean" policy at about NIS 2,000 a year with reduced compensation for disability and loss of earning capacity, alongside a full, dearer policy [S] — [Ynet](https://www.ynet.co.il/wheels/article/5279576); 2007 and 2008 bills proposed rider deductibles limited to pain and suffering [S] — [Walla cars](https://cars.walla.co.il/item/1243562); a 2018 public committee's cost review was reported to imply a 20% rise for motorcycles and 15% for cars [S] — [Walla finance](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3132610). A 2026 district court declined to cut an uninsured rider's pain-and-suffering award, saying such a change "requires legislation" [P] — [ICE, 28.10.2026](https://www.ice.co.il/bdispot/news/article/1135504).
- **E-bikes and e-kick-scooters:** they are not treated as motor vehicles for compulsory insurance (the Supreme Court is reported to have ruled that e-bikes are not "motor vehicles"; a pedestrian hit by a cyclist is not a "road accident" under the compensation law); bills by MKs Margi and Asher sought mandatory or voluntary insurance and plates; voluntary personal-accident and third-party products exist (for example from about NIS 70). [S] — [Walla finance](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3610318); [ICE](https://www.ice.co.il/auto/news/article/1026777). The Pool stopped insuring Segways and mobility scooters in 2022 because they do not meet the definition of a motor vehicle. [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, §1.4.5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- [I] Protective-gear discounts are not among the approved compulsory rating variables I found; "motorcycle safety systems" refers to vehicle systems (ABS and similar), not rider gear.
- [I] The model relativities show where the Pool tariff is most likely furthest below cost: riders under 30, licence seniority under 4 years, no deductible, engines above 250cc. Illustrative model-implied pure premiums (constant × factors; my arithmetic): age 17–22, new licence, no deductible, ≤125cc ≈ NIS 14,700; same rider on 251–345cc ≈ NIS 31,100; age 23–29, 4–9 years, with deductible, ≤125cc ≈ NIS 3,800; age 40–45, 10+ years, with deductible, 126–250cc ≈ NIS 4,100; age 52–59, 10+ years, with deductible, 126–250cc ≈ NIS 1,700; age 60+, 10+ years, no deductible, above 995cc ≈ NIS 4,800; age 60+, with deductible, ≤125cc ≈ NIS 1,000. These are unsmoothed and exclude expenses and profit.
- [I] The enacted deductible (NIS 25,000 on non-pecuniary loss, 7 days' earnings) cuts expected cost far more than its 30% price discount for private riders (cost with deductible NIS 4,665 versus 8,572 without, i.e. −46%; the GLM factor is 0.61), which signals strong self-selection: careful riders choose the deductible.

### Gaps
- Whether the joint Knesset committee approved mileage and the motorcycle training-course variable between September 2025 and November 2026 is unknown.
- The outcome of the 2018 "lean policy" committee and any 2023–2026 bill to move two-wheelers out of the no-fault law, cap benefits or create first-party-only cover could not be established; no source reports enactment.
- The Pool tariff's actual age and seniority factors (to compare against the model relativities) were not obtained.

## 7. Comparison anchors: what riders pay for mandatory motorcycle liability elsewhere, and how the covered benefit differs

### Takeaway
Typical mandatory motorcycle liability premiums in reference countries are in the low hundreds of euros, pounds or dollars a year (Italy about €300–355 for motorcycles and €200–236 for mopeds; France about €370 for third-party; Spain about €150–180; German mopeds €30–70; UK about £275 on average; US minimum liability roughly $140–$365), against Israel's average of about NIS 3,800 and base tariffs of NIS 3,400–8,000. The products are not like-for-like: elsewhere the mandatory cover pays only third parties, whereas Israel's compulsory policy also pays the rider's own bodily injury regardless of fault, which is where most of the cost sits.

### Cited Findings
- **Italy (IVASS price survey, RC = mandatory liability):** average premium for motorcycles €311 (Q1 2025), €302 (Q2 2025), €329 (Q3 2025), €355 (Q4 2025, +11.3% year on year), €321 (Q2 2026, +6.1%); mopeds €205, €196, €211, €236 for the same 2025 quarters; premiums are higher in the South. Another observatory gives €306 for 2025 (+5.6%). [S] — [Segugio.it](https://assicurazioni.segugio.it/news-assicurazioni/cresce-il-costo-medio-dell-rc-auto-3-7-nel-secondo-trimestre-2025.html); [SicurAuto](https://www.sicurauto.it/news/attualita-e-curiosita/rc-auto-premio-medio-2025-4-ancora-un-anno-di-rincari/). A trade headline contrasts "RC moto €289 in Italy versus €189 in France". [S, headline] — [inSella](https://www.insella.it/news/italia-rc-moto-costa-289-euro-francia-189-perche-67815)
- **France (au tiers = third-party):** average €367 a year for a motorcycle and €427 for a scooter (January–May 2025), €371 per LeLynx, 2025 range €362–418 (+5–6%); Groupama's rough guide €300–350; all-formula regional averages run from €691 to €1,011. [S] — [LeComparateurAssurance](https://www.lecomparateurassurance.com/assurance-moto/comparatif/meilleure); [LeLynx](https://lelynx.fr/assurance-moto/); [Groupama](https://www.groupama.fr/assurance-moto/conseils/prix/)
- **Spain:** extended third-party (terceros ampliado) averaged €150–180 a year in 2025 for standard urban profiles; basic third-party quotes from about €153; brand averages €151–438 in a 2026 ranking. [S] — [Acierto](https://www.acierto.com/seguros-moto/terceros-ampliado/); [Rastreator](https://www.rastreator.com/seguros-de-moto/analisis/motos-mas-baratas-mas-caras-asegurar)
- **Germany:** mopeds and small scooters on an annual insurance plate (Versicherungskennzeichen) pay about €30–70 a year for liability (comparison portal from €25.67 for a part-year), young riders more; no average for full-size motorcycle liability was found (one tariff example: €56.33 for a Honda Rebel 500; one insurer reports an average motorcycle liability claim of about €7,000 versus €5,000 for all vehicles). [S] — [versicherungsriese.de](https://www.versicherungsriese.de/versicherungskennzeichen/mopedversicherung/); [CHECK24](https://www.check24.de/mopedversicherung/fragen/deckungssumme); [R+V press release, 26.5.2025](https://www.ruv.de/newsroom/pressemitteilungen/2025-05-26-biker-unfallschaeden)
- **United Kingdom:** average motorbike insurance £273 (Confused.com, August–October 2025, all cover types); £279 for about 1,000 miles a year (Lexham, Q4 2025); third-party-only for 25-year-olds averages £480 with cheapest quotes around £254 (NimbleFins, 2026). [S] — [Confused.com](https://www.confused.com/motorbike-insurance/faqs); [Lexham](https://www.lexhaminsurance.co.uk/blog/how-can-i-save-money-on-my-motorcycle-insurance/); [NimbleFins](https://www.nimblefins.co.uk/node/386)
- **United States:** minimum-liability policies average about $141 a year nationally (MoneyGeek, 2025); Progressive's state averages for liability-only range from $151 (Pennsylvania) to $365 (Delaware) a year; one aggregator reports $977 for liability-only (outlier). [S] — [MoneyGeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/motorcycle/22-year-old/); [Progressive](https://www.progressive.com/answers/motorcycle-insurance-cost-factors/); [Insurify](https://insurify.com/specialty-vehicles/motorcycles/cheap/)
- **Benefit difference (US, authoritative):** "Liability insurance covers bodily injury and property damage that you may cause to other people involved in an accident. It doesn't cover injury to you or damage to your motorcycle"; passenger (guest) liability and uninsured-motorist cover are separate options; most states require only minimum liability. [P] — [Insurance Information Institute, "Motorcycle insurance"](https://www.iii.org/article/motorcycle-insurance)
- **Israel for contrast:** the compulsory policy covers bodily injury only (no property), but for the rider, pillion and pedestrians, without regard to fault; average net premium NIS 3,795 (2024) and expected cost NIS 8,149 per motorcycle-year. [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf); [Calcalist, 15.5.2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/article/ryrlti1s2). The Israeli Motorcycle Club's lawyer argued in 2018 that insurance cost explains the low number of two-wheelers versus Greece or Italy. [P] — [Calcalist, 21.2.2018](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3732436,00.html)

### Inferences
- [I] Even before currency conversion the gap is roughly an order of magnitude: Israeli riders pay several thousand shekels for the mandatory policy where European riders pay a few hundred euros. At any plausible exchange rate (about 3.5–4.2 NIS per euro; I did not verify the November 2026 rate) Italy's €300–355 is about NIS 1,100–1,500, i.e. about a third of Israel's subsidised average and about a sixth of its unsubsidised cost.
- [I] The gap is mainly a coverage difference, not an efficiency difference: Israel's NIS 11,000 "true" cost per motorcycle-year is dominated by the rider's own injuries (single-vehicle and at-fault accidents are paid in full), which mandatory third-party liability abroad does not cover. A like-for-like comparison would add voluntary rider personal-accident cover, health and disability insurance and social security abroad.
- [I] Background knowledge, not verified in this session: in the EU, the UK and most US states an at-fault or single-vehicle rider recovers nothing for own injuries from the mandatory motor policy; France's 1985 Badinter law protects non-driver victims but lets a driver's own fault reduce or exclude his own claim.

### Gaps
- All foreign price points are from comparison sites or trade press via search excerpts; no regulator or insurer-association source (IVASS, GDV, ABI, France Assureurs, UNESPA, NAIC) was read directly, and the average for German full-size motorcycles is missing.
- No source quantifies how much a European or US rider pays in total once voluntary first-party injury cover is added, so no true like-for-like price exists in these notes.
- No international data on uninsured-riding rates were collected to compare with Israel's 45%.

## 8. Could a private insurer profitably offer lower premiums than the Pool?

### Takeaway
On average, no: the Pool's price is about half of expected claims even after the 75% recovery from car insurers (loss ratio 184–199% before expenses), a private insurer's compulsory tariff must sit below the Pool's, and the market has accordingly shrunk to 112 privately insured motorcycle-years. The only room for a profitable private offer is selective — rider segments whose true cost is below the Pool tariff (the operator's own model points to older, experienced riders with a deductible, and its data show named-rider company-owned bikes with a deductible at a 74% loss ratio) — or the unregulated property lines.

### Cited Findings
- Loss ratio of motorcycle compulsory insurance at Pool prices: 181% (2020–2022 experience), 184% (2024 forecast), 199% projected for 2025 after the 8% tariff cut; underwriting loss about −83% of premium for underwriting year 2024 even after discounting claims. [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, Tables 1003, 1001A, 1007](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- A private insurer keeps the same 75% splitting-order recovery in mixed accidents (it is paid to "the insurer of the motorcycle"), so the relevant cost benchmark for it is the post-recovery NIS 6,978 (excluding NII) per average motorcycle-year, not NIS 11,000. [P] — [Commissioner's report for 2024, footnote 11](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/report_0010/he/Commisioner%20Report%20Design%20B_chapter5_%20ver%206.0_digital.pdf); [Statistical operator report 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- Price ceiling: insurers' maximum compulsory tariffs depend on Pool prices and must be lower (about 90%); private quotes of "NIS 5,000 and more" for some motorcycles are reported where the Pool charges about half. [P] — [Globes, 16.3.2023](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001441430)
- Segment evidence: the one sub-group with premium above expected cost in 2024 is company-owned motorcycles with a named rider and a deductible — premium NIS 3,575 versus expected cost NIS 2,657 (74%), 3,609 exposure-years; the operator explains these are effectively private riders classified as "company" for tax reasons and warns the group is small. Every other sub-group runs at 154–207%. [P] — [Statistical operator report 2024, Table 30 and §8.2.2](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- Risk gradient available for selection: age 52–59 and 60+ relativities of 0.22 and 0.18 versus 1.00 for ages 17–22; 10+ years' seniority 0.61; deductible 0.61; an accident in the record ×1.56; engine above 685cc ×3.0. The operator adds a +3% correction for misdeclared rider age, i.e. under-declaration is a known problem. [P] — [same report, Tables 301 and 308](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- Price sensitivity of demand: the 10% increase of July 2023 cut the insured count 4–5% below expectation, and the operator assumes the 8% cut of January 2025 adds 4% exposure; 45–46% of machines are uninsured. [P] — [same report, §11.4 and Table 45](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf)
- Commentary in favour of risk-based pricing: Globes argued (2009) that the insurance cost should be fully reflected in a rider's purchase decision and that congestion or environmental goals should be pursued through tax benefits rather than insurance subsidies. [P] — [Globes, 5.10.2009](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1000502519)

### Inferences
- [I] Break-even arithmetic: to cover expected claims alone a private insurer would need about NIS 7,000 per average motorcycle-year (excluding NII), 1.8 times the Pool's average NIS 3,795, plus expenses, commissions and cost of capital — while being required to price below the Pool. Undercutting the Pool across the board is therefore impossible without a subsidy; the Pool's deficit is funded by a statutory levy that a private insurer writing motorcycles outside the Pool would not receive on its own book.
- [I] Selective undercutting is arithmetically possible wherever the Pool tariff exceeds a segment's cost. Using the model, an experienced 52+ rider with a deductible on a scooter up to 250cc has a model-implied pure premium of roughly NIS 1,000–1,700, well below the NIS 2,886 average paid by private riders with a deductible. Whether the Pool tariff already gives such riders comparably steep discounts is unknown (tariff age factors not obtained), so the size of this niche cannot be confirmed.
- [I] Cream-skimming would not shrink the system's cost: every good risk removed from the Pool raises the Pool's loss ratio, and the loading ceiling (8%) would then force higher Pool tariffs for the remaining (younger, heavier-bike) riders or a higher ceiling — a policy consequence regulators are likely to weigh.
- [I] Levers that could lower true cost rather than shift it: verified mileage and telematics (per-km cost is roughly constant at about NIS 0.94 per km, so low-mileage riders are cheaper), a verified training course, power-based rating, and a larger deductible (riders with the existing deductible cost 46% less). Only the deductible is currently usable; mileage and the training course were still awaiting Knesset-committee approval at the last date I could verify (September 2025).
- [I] The uninsured half of the fleet (about 75 thousand vehicle-years) is not an easy growth pool: at average cost each newly insured rider adds about NIS 7,000 of claims against about NIS 3,800 of premium.

### Gaps
- No financial data on any private insurer's motorcycle compulsory book (112 vehicle-years is too small to analyse), and no information on expense ratios for motorcycle business.
- No data on whether the Authority would approve a private motorcycle tariff using stricter underwriting, or on any insurer or insurtech application to do so in 2025–2026.
- No profitability data for motorcycle comprehensive/third-party property business, where pricing is free.
