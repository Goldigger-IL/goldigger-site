# Israeli private-car motor insurance market — size, prices, claims and insurer profitability (state as of November 2026)

**How to read these notes**

- Three products are kept separate throughout: compulsory / ביטוח חובה ("רכב חובה", bodily injury only, under the 1975 Road Accident Victims Compensation Law / חוק הפלת"ד); comprehensive / ביטוח מקיף; third-party property / צד ג'. The regulator's statistics merge the last two into one line, "motor property" / רכב רכוש (comprehensive + TP-only), and split them only for vehicle and claim counts.
- Evidence tags on every item: **[PR]** = page-read (I read the full text of the page or PDF); **[SE]** = search-excerpt only (the search engine's summary; the page itself was not opened — treat numbers as needing verification); **[INF]** = my own calculation or inference.
- Tooling note for the report writer: the WebFetch tool failed for every host in this session (DNS error), including the "allowed" ones. Pages on the allowed hosts (gov.il BlobFolder PDFs, cbs.gov.il, calcalist, globes, en.globes, bizportal, ice) were instead read by direct HTTP download and text extraction. gov.il HTML pages are behind a bot wall, so only gov.il PDFs were read. Knesset Research Center documents (fs.knesset.gov.il), insurers' own financial statements (pr.harel-group.co.il, lbr.co.il, aig.co.il), pool.org.il, funder.co.il, walla, maariv and kolzchut could not be opened; anything from them is [SE] or second-hand via press. Globes pages are partly paywalled; the visible text was read.
- Regulator = Capital Market, Insurance and Savings Authority (רשות שוק ההון, ביטוח וחיסכון, "CMA"); its annual "Commissioner's Report" (דוח הממונה) for 2025 was published around September 2026 and is the main primary source. "Menbara" (מנברא) is the operator of the statutory compulsory-insurance statistical database (מאגר המידע הסטטיסטי).
- All money in NIS; "m" = million, "bn" = billion (מיליארד).

## 1. Market size: premiums, insured vehicles and average premium per vehicle (2019–2026)

### Takeaway
Israelis paid about NIS 20.8bn for car insurance in 2025: NIS 14.05bn motor property (comprehensive + TP) and NIS 6.74bn compulsory; motor property premiums jumped about 60% between 2021 and 2024 on only ~12% more insured vehicles and then flattened in 2025. The average motor-property premium per insured vehicle rose from about NIS 2,783 (2021) to NIS 3,962 (2024) and stayed there in 2025, while the average compulsory premium for a private car rose every year from NIS 1,339 (2021) to NIS 1,756 (2025); market price indices for comprehensive cover have fallen roughly 25–29% from the early-2025 peak by autumn 2026, with compulsory still rising.

### Inferences
- Average motor property premium per insured vehicle, my calculation (premiums ÷ insured vehicles): 2021 ≈ NIS 2,783 (8,717m ÷ 3.132m, which reproduces the Knesset figure, so the Knesset series starts in 2021 and the chart label "3,250" for 2021 is probably wrong); 2022 ≈ 2,974; 2023 ≈ 3,508; 2024 ≈ 3,962; 2025 ≈ 3,956 (14,052m ÷ 3.552m). So +42% from 2021 to 2024, then flat in 2025 [INF]. This average mixes comprehensive and TP-only, private and fleet vehicles; a comprehensive-only average would be higher.
- The flat 2025 average despite falling quoted prices is expected: written premiums lag quotes by up to a year, the CBS/Wobi indices track new quotes, and the big cuts came in H2 2025 and 2026 [INF].
- The frequently quoted "60%" rise matches the growth of total motor property premiums 2021→2024 (8,717m → 13,906m = +59.5%), not the per-vehicle price, which rose ~42% over the same years [INF].
- Compulsory per private car: +31% nominal from 2021 (1,339) to 2025 (1,756); +30% from ~1,355 in 2019 [INF].
- Combined out-of-pocket for a car with both covers at industry averages: ≈ NIS 4,120 in 2021 → ≈ NIS 5,650 in 2024 → ≈ NIS 5,710 in 2025 (sum of the two averages above; rough, because the property average includes TP-only vehicles) [INF].

### Gaps
- No official 2026 premium totals by line exist yet (the CMA annual report for 2026 is due around September 2027); quarterly CMA statistics were not located.
- Motor property premiums for 2019–2020 were not retrieved (the 2019–2021 CMA reports were not opened), so the per-vehicle average for 2019–2020 is missing.
- No official split of premiums or average price between comprehensive and TP-only; no official private-car-only average for comprehensive.
- The Knesset Research Center papers of December 2025 and March 2026 could not be opened (host blocked); their figures are quoted second-hand.

## 2. Profitability by line and by insurer; return on equity

### Takeaway
Motor property swung from a NIS ~1.55bn loss in 2022 (loss ratio 92%) to record profits: NIS 1.79bn pre-tax in 2024 and NIS 2.12bn in 2025 (loss ratio 64%, about 15% of premiums), which the regulator itself called "exceptional profitability"; over the full 2021–2025 cycle the line earned only about 4% of premiums. Compulsory motor is structurally thin: actuarial loss ratios around or above 100%, underwriting losses at some insurers, and a total pre-tax profit of NIS 456m (2024) and NIS 481m (2025) that depends on investment income. No source publishes a return on equity for the motor lines; whole-insurer ROE was 29.6% in 2025 (a decade high, helped by capital markets and IFRS 17) after −1.2% in 2022, averaging about 12% over 2016–2025.

### Inferences
- Pre-tax margin on written premiums, motor property: 1,787 ÷ 13,906 = 12.9% (2024); 2,120 ÷ 14,052 = 15.1% (2025). Underwriting margin ≈ 1.5 ÷ 13.9 = ~11% (2024) and 1.8 ÷ 14.05 = ~13% (2025), implying an industry combined ratio of roughly 87–89%, consistent with the 84–90% reported by the large insurers [INF].
- Compulsory motor: 456 ÷ 6,576 = 6.9% (2024) and 481 ÷ 6,741 = 7.1% (2025) pre-tax on premiums, earned on a long-tail book where investment income matters; the CMA text confirms the underwriting engine of the segment is motor property, and Harel's NIS 173m compulsory underwriting loss in 2024 shows individual insurers can still lose on it [INF].
- Full-cycle view for motor property using the press series plus CMA figures: 2021 (−30m) + 2022 (−1,550m) + 2023 (+124m) + 2024 (+1,787m) + 2025 (+2,120m) = +NIS 2.45bn on NIS 58.7bn of premiums = about 4.2% of premiums over five years, versus 15% in 2025 alone. Whether returns are "very high" therefore depends on the window: peak years 2024–2025 are far above the 2010–2021 norm of ~NIS 260m a year; the five-year average is modest [INF].
- Sector-wide ROE averaged about 12% over 2016–2025 (using the old-standard 17.0% for 2024); 2025's 29.6% is partly a product of equity-market gains and the IFRS 17 transition, so it cannot be read as the return on motor business [INF].
- No published figure isolates capital allocated to motor lines, so "return on capital of motor insurance" can only be bracketed: thin-to-negative in 2021–2023, and probably well above the cost of capital in 2024–2025 given 13–15% pre-tax margins on premiums and the 28–47% ROE of the two motor-focused insurers [INF].
- The steep 2026 erosion (Migdal into loss, Phoenix −21%, Ayalon −32% in the segment's quarterly profit) suggests 2024–2025 was the top of the cycle [INF].

## 3. What compulsory insurance covers, who pays medical costs, and how the design raises cost

### Takeaway
Compulsory insurance is a no-fault, absolute-liability bodily-injury scheme covering the driver (including an at-fault driver), passengers and pedestrians, with statutory caps on earnings loss and pain-and-suffering; it also works as a collection channel: about a quarter of the net premium is transferred by law to the health funds, the National Insurance Institute and the Karnit fund, and drivers additionally cross-subsidise motorcyclists through the Pool. The "9.4%" transfer for medical care is outdated: it was raised to 12.66% from 1 January 2022.

### Cited Findings
- Scope: covers bodily injury (including psychological harm) from a road accident to the driver, the passengers and pedestrians hit by the vehicle; it does not cover damage to the insured car or to others' property, which needs TP or comprehensive cover. Liability is absolute, regardless of fault: "a driver who caused the accident himself is entitled to compensation for his own bodily injury from his car's compulsory policy, exactly like the passenger next to him and the pedestrian". The policy attaches to the vehicle and covers anyone driving with permission, subject to restrictions on the certificate (e.g. minimum driver age) — [Bizportal guide, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.bizportal.co.il/guides/news/article/20038739) [PR]; Kol Zchut: entitlement is unrelated to who was at fault — [Kol Zchut](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%AA%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A2%D7%94_%D7%9C%D7%A7%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%AA_%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%99%D7%99%D7%9D_%D7%91%D7%92%D7%99%D7%9F_%D7%A0%D7%96%D7%A7%D7%99_%D7%92%D7%95%D7%A3_%D7%91%D7%AA%D7%90%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%AA_%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%99%D7%9D) [SE].
- Contributory negligence of the victim is not taken into account — [Hebrew University law journal (Mishpatim) article](https://lawjournal.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/2018-03/%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%A6%D7%95%D7%99%D7%99%D7%9D%20%D7%91%D7%92%D7%99%D7%9F%20%D7%A0%D7%96%D7%A7%D7%99%20%D7%92%D7%95%D7%A3%20-%20%D7%93%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%99%20%D7%94%D7%A0%D7%96%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%9F%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%9C%20%D7%91%D7%99%D7%98%D7%95%D7%97%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%90%D7%9C%D7%99.pdf) [SE].
- Karnit (קרנית, Road Accident Victims Compensation Fund) pays victims of hit-and-run and of uninsured vehicles; an uninsured driver gets nothing, and driving uninsured is a criminal offence — [Bizportal guide, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.bizportal.co.il/guides/news/article/20038739) [PR]. Karnit is funded by a fixed 1% of net compulsory premiums — [Menbara 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf) [PR]; its premiums were NIS 60m (2023), NIS 65m (2024) and NIS 71m (2025) — [CMA Report 2025 ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf) [PR].
- Caps: loss of earning capacity is limited to three times the average wage in the economy; non-pecuniary damages (pain and suffering) are capped — a 2014 Knesset preparatory document gives NIS 170,000, to be updated by order; losses are computed net of income tax (1977 amendment); for a death, the estate receives 25% of the statutory maximum — [Knesset committee document, 2014](https://production.oknesset.org/pipelines/data/committees/download_document_committee_session/files/87/3/0/307904.DOC) [SE]; [original 1975 law text](https://fs.knesset.gov.il/8/law/8_lsr_209261.pdf) [SE]; [court decision, 2018](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/dynamiccollectorresultitem/decision11873-02-18/he/11873-02-18.pdf) [SE]. The current indexed cap was not confirmed.
- Medical costs: under the Economic Efficiency Law for 2009–2010 responsibility for medical treatment of road-accident victims moved to the health funds (קופות החולים); insurers transfer a fixed share of compulsory premiums through Karnit, and the National Insurance Institute distributes it among the funds — [Government decision 139 of 2009](https://www.gov.il/he/pages/2009_des139) [SE]. The rate was 9.4% (set in 2009) and was raised to 12.66% of compulsory premiums collected in the previous month from 1 January 2022; insurers then raised compulsory prices 3–5% with CMA approval — [Funder, pre-2022](https://www.funder.co.il/article/118520) [SE]; [Funder, 2022](https://www.funder.co.il/article/129082) [SE]. Menbara's list of statutory tariff loadings confirms "medical expenses 12.66% of net premium plus fees" — [Menbara 2024, s. 1.6.8.6](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf) [PR].
- National Insurance Institute (המוסד לביטוח לאומי): until 2022 the Institute recovered from insurers, case by case, the benefits it paid to road-accident victims (subrogation, s. 328). Section 328A (added by the 2018 Economic Efficiency Law, in force from accident year 2023) replaced this with a fixed transfer: 10% of premiums (net premium plus fees) in 2023–2024 and 10.95% from 2025. Menbara estimates the Institute received 10.6% of pre-reduction net premium in 2024 and gave up claims worth 11.0% of discounted claims (at a 3% discount assumption) — [Menbara 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf) [PR]; [Menbara 2022, ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2022.pd_.pdf) [PR].
- Other costs the tariff must carry beyond pure risk: indirect claim-handling costs, marketing and agents' commissions, overheads, cost of capital under Solvency II, and reinsurance — [Menbara 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf) [PR].
- Motorcycle cross-subsidy through the Pool (הפול, the residual insurer owned jointly by the insurers, whose tariffs are set by the Commissioner): (a) when a motorcycle and another vehicle collide, the other vehicle's insurer pays 75% of the rider's bodily-injury compensation (50% before 2008); (b) Pool losses are loaded on all compulsory policyholders up to 8% of the branch's pure risk cost. In 2025 motorcycle claims in the Pool cost ~NIS 723m against riders' premiums of NIS 365m, with ~NIS 268m expected from the 75% rule and a public subsidy of ~NIS 90m (NIS 208m in 2024, NIS 240m in 2023, NIS 336m in 2022). Average subsidy per insured vehicle: NIS 116 (2018), 111 (2019), 95 (2020), 91 (2021), 87 (2022), 64 (2023), 53 (2024), 23 (2025; reduced by a one-off deal with the National Insurance Institute and an accounting change) — [CMA Report 2025 ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf) [PR]; [CMA Report 2023 ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/report_0006/he/The_Commissioner%27s_Report_Chapter_5.pdf.pdf) [PR]; [Menbara 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf) [PR].
- For private cars the Pool loading moves the expected loss ratio from 99% to 107% (2024 basis) — [Menbara 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf) [PR].
- The Knesset Research Center notes that Israeli compulsory cover is broader than in some EU countries, "so it is not a uniform product", when comparing price trends with Europe — [Globes, 16 Dec 2025](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001529202) [PR, second-hand].
- Claim tail: bodily-injury claims can take five to six years to assess and pay, so liabilities are discounted and results are sensitive to interest rates — [Globes, 14 Dec 2023](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001465106) [PR].
- Average compensation in 2023: about NIS 1.266m for a two-wheeler rider versus about NIS 330,000 for a car driver or passenger — [Walla Finance](https://finance.walla.co.il/item/3749623) [SE; basis unclear, probably serious claims only].

### Inferences
- Statutory transfers embedded in the 2025 compulsory tariff add up to roughly 1% (Karnit) + 12.66% (health funds) + 10.95% (National Insurance) ≈ 24–25% of net premium, before any Pool loading (up to 8% of pure risk cost). A material part of the Israeli compulsory premium is therefore a hypothecated levy for public health and social insurance, which fault-based liability systems typically do not bundle in this way [INF].
- Design features that raise cost relative to fault-based third-party liability: (1) first-party cover for the at-fault driver and for single-vehicle accidents; (2) no reduction for the victim's own negligence; (3) passengers and pedestrians paid without proof of fault; (4) uncapped medical care, funded by the percentage levy; (5) the motorcycle cross-subsidy. Features that lower cost: caps on earnings (3× average wage) and on pain and suffering, no punitive damages and less litigation over liability [INF].
- Because the earnings cap and non-pecuniary cap are linked to the average wage and CPI, bodily-injury severity rises automatically with wages and inflation; Menbara explicitly models claims trend from average-wage growth [INF from Menbara PR].

### Gaps
- The current indexed maximum for non-pecuniary damages and the exact statutory wording of the three-times-average-wage rule could not be read from a primary legal text (Nevo/Kol Zchut not accessible; three searches returned no current figure).
- Whether 12.66% is still the health-fund rate in November 2026, and the exact base (premiums collected vs net premium), were not verified against the current order; the brief's "~9.4%" matches the pre-2022 rate.
- No source quantifies what share of compulsory claim cost goes to at-fault drivers or single-vehicle accidents, so the cost of the no-fault design relative to a fault system is not quantified.
- Legal costs and lawyers' fees in compulsory claims were not sourced.

## 4. Cost drivers specific to Israel

### Takeaway
The 2021–2024 surge had identifiable causes: a post-COVID parts-price shock amplified by a rule obliging appraisers to value damage at importers' list prices, a theft wave that peaked around 2023–2024 (roughly 18,000 vehicles in 2024, about 1 in 200), rising accident costs and expensive-to-repair electric and Chinese models; since 2025 thefts and parts prices have eased, which, with regulatory pressure, is pulling comprehensive prices down. In compulsory cover the drivers are wage- and CPI-linked bodily-injury severity, a shift toward large claims, and interest rates used to discount long-tail reserves.

### Cited Findings

**Car theft**

- Police data: 5,527 theft events in 2019, over 6,000 in 2021, 6,786 in 2022, over 2,000 in Q1 2023 — [Calcalist, 2 May 2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/hjv4fztq3) [PR]. Police data obtained by Globes under freedom of information: 10,644 thefts in H1 2023 and 8,447 in H1 2024 (−21%) — [Globes, 23 Jul 2024](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001484866) [PR].
- About 18,000 vehicles stolen in 2024, "one in every 200"; close to 40,000 stolen in 2023–2024 together, 35% more than in the previous two years; ~4,200 in Q1 2025 — [Calcalist, 27 Nov 2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/rkbh00hnbwl) [PR]; [Calcalist, 7 Apr 2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/market/article/r1ashgxcjx) [PR].
- 2025: about 15,400 thefts expected for the year, −15%, the lowest in four years — [Bizportal, 22 Dec 2025](https://www.bizportal.co.il/Insurance/news/article/20025225) [PR]. Tracking firms: Pointer −18% in thefts and attempts, Ituran −21%, the first fall after four years of increases, but Q4 2025 was +25% on Q3 after the war ended — [Globes, 6 Jan 2026](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001531135) [PR].
- 2026: official police figures show 6,240 vehicles stolen in H1 2026 versus 7,926 in H1 2025 (−21%); 4,006 of the 2026 total were private cars (the rest motorcycles, trucks, tractors). Police attribute the fall to "preventive actions", including security upgrades by importers and manufacturers. Tracking firms count attempts on their own subscribers only and report different trends — [Calcalist, 30 Jun 2026](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/rj11ucxw7zx) [PR].
- Knesset Research Center: thefts rose 50% between 2021 and 2022 and 25% between 2022 and 2023, from 3,910 in 2020 to 10,536 in 2023, and their cost to insurers rose from NIS 237m to NIS 963m — [Globes, 6 Jan 2026](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001531135) [PR, second-hand]. This count conflicts with the police series above (10,644 in half of 2023 alone); the cost figures suggest the Knesset numbers are insured theft claims [INF].
- Thefts account for about 15% of insurers' claims cost in motor property; the bulk is accidents — [Calcalist, 7 Apr 2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/market/article/r1ashgxcjx) [PR]. Industry estimate of damage NIS 2–2.5bn a year, at an average stolen-vehicle value of NIS 95,000 — [Globes English, 25 Feb 2024](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-israels-car-theft-plague-returns-1001471962) [PR].
- Destination: Ituran estimates ~52% of stolen vehicles go to the parts trade and ~48% are sold in Palestinian Authority territory and in Israel — [Globes, 6 Jan 2026](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001531135) [PR]. Thefts collapsed after 7 October 2023 because of movement restrictions in the West Bank, then returned — [Globes English, 25 Feb 2024](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-israels-car-theft-plague-returns-1001471962) [PR]. Pointer attributes the 2025 fall to tighter border-fence security and the ban on entry of Palestinian workers — [Globes, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001516240) [PR].
- By type in 2025 (Pointer): electric-car thefts −50%, SUVs −18%, hybrids +22%, motorcycles +80% and a fifth of all thefts; SUVs/crossovers ~47% and private cars ~32% of thefts, mainly Toyota and Hyundai — [Globes, 6 Jan 2026](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001531135) [PR]; [Bizportal, 22 Dec 2025](https://www.bizportal.co.il/Insurance/news/article/20025225) [PR].

**Repair, parts, appraisers and arrangement garages (מוסכי הסדר)**

- "Repair cost in garages is 70% of total premium, so parts prices are critical" — senior deputy commissioner Ohad Maodi to the Knesset Economic Affairs Committee, February 2023 — [Calcalist, 2 May 2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/hjv4fztq3) [PR].
- Transport Ministry guidelines from 2015 oblige vehicle appraisers (שמאים) to value damage at official importer price lists, which the State Comptroller found can be up to double real market prices; garages buy at a discount and bill insurers at list. Appraisers must also use the garage's own hourly rate — [Bizportal, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.bizportal.co.il/car/news/article/20028281) [PR]; [Calcalist, 2 May 2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/hjv4fztq3) [PR].
- About 50% of insured repairs were done outside arrangement garages, at an estimated NIS 3–3.5bn (2023) — [Calcalist, 2 May 2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/hjv4fztq3) [PR].
- Insurers (Phoenix, Clal and others), with a 2023 ruling by the Commissioner, began deducting the gap between list price and market price for customers who repair outside arrangement garages; the Supreme Court rejected the Garage Association's petition against the CMA in February 2026, upholding the practice subject to explicit disclosure in the policy and at claim time — [Bizportal, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.bizportal.co.il/car/news/article/20028281) [PR]; [CMA Report 2025 ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf) [PR].
- A regulation change letting appraisers use market prices was due in May 2025, but Transport Minister Miri Regev declined to sign it under pressure from garages; the regulations have been stuck for more than a year — [Calcalist, 27 Nov 2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/rkbh00hnbwl) [PR]; [Bizportal, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.bizportal.co.il/car/news/article/20028281) [PR].
- Total-loss dynamics: a car is a constructive total loss when gross damage exceeds 50–60% of value; inflated list prices push repairable cars into total loss. Since 2022 the standard policy lets the insured choose repair instead; constructive total losses have fallen since 2023 and actual total losses fell in 2025 for the first time in many years, but the CMA says list-price valuation "still takes more vehicles off the road" — [Calcalist, 2 May 2023](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/hjv4fztq3) [PR]; [CMA Report 2025 ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf) [PR].
- Parts prices jumped in 2022–2024 (supply-chain disruption, global inflation) and have stabilised; garage labour costs are still rising in 2026 because of a shortage of technicians — [Bizportal, 3 Nov 2026](https://www.bizportal.co.il/Insurance/news/article/20047090) [PR]; [ice, 2 Nov 2026](https://www.ice.co.il/auto/news/article/1136087) [PR]. "Israel has a severe parts problem and very many car models … sometimes a car is off the road for a long time after an accident because there are no parts" (CEO of comparison site Besti) — [Globes, 6 Jan 2026](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001531135) [PR].

**Electric, hybrid and Chinese-brand vehicles**

- Insurer data given to Calcalist: accident frequency of electric cars is 40–45% higher than petrol cars; average repair cost 35–45% higher (expensive components, battery in the floor, more parts per accident); theft frequency about five times higher; batteries are 60–70% of the car's value; a policy for an electric car "should be about 50% dearer" than for a petrol car of similar value; some insurers cut the number of electric cars they insure. Wobi: Geely model +20% and MG ZS hybrid +16% in a year when average prices were flat — [Calcalist, 7 Apr 2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/market/article/r1ashgxcjx) [PR].
- Importer parts price lists for Chinese electric cars: a Geely battery listed at NIS 128,550; comprehensive premiums for some electric cars rose from ~NIS 4,000 to over NIS 7,000 in 2023 — [Calcalist, parts prices](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/hkvjb8jvgx) [SE]; [Calcalist, EV insurance](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/b1wcq1fe6) [SE].
- Menbara (compulsory): international studies indicate bodily-injury claim frequency for electric cars may be up to 40% higher than for comparable combustion cars, with similar severity; Israeli experience is still insufficient — [Menbara 2022](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2022.pd_.pdf) [PR].

**Vehicle taxation and car values**

- Globes: the rise in insurance tariffs in recent years is "attributed mainly to an accelerated rise in the average transaction price of new cars in Israel, stemming from accelerated tax increases and external industry factors"; average transaction prices fell in H1 2025 with discounts and "zero-kilometre" sales, pulling insurance prices down — [Globes, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001516240) [PR].
- Purchase tax (מס קנייה) on non-electric private cars is 83%; on electric cars sources conflict between 45% and 83% from January 2025 — [Globes](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001493597) [SE]; [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local/articles/0,7340,L-3764446,00.html) [SE].

**Bodily-injury claim inflation (compulsory)**

- Frequency of small claims is falling while frequency and severity of claims above NIS 100,000 are rising; total risk cost rose. The CMA lists as causes: rising treatment costs, court rulings and precedents under the Compensation Law, the rising average wage and CPI — [CMA Report 2023 ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/report_0006/he/The_Commissioner%27s_Report_Chapter_5.pdf.pdf) [PR]; [CMA Report 2024 ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/report_0010/he/Commisioner%20Report%20Design%20B_chapter5_%20ver%206.0_digital.pdf) [PR]. In the last four years frequency and severity have stabilised, so average cost now moves with CPI — [CMA Report 2025 ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf) [PR].
- Closed-claim study, all vehicle types (January 2021 prices): claims closed per year fell from 97,245 (2012) to 60,860 (2022); frequency from 4.30% to 1.82%; average cost per closed claim rose from NIS 34,099 to NIS 74,758; claims above NIS 1m closed: 394 (2012), 369 (2020), 570 (2022), averaging ~NIS 2.3–2.5m each — [Menbara 2022, Table 74](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2022.pd_.pdf) [PR]. Frequency of NIS 100k–1m claims rose from 0.24% to 0.32% — [Menbara 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf) [PR].

**Interest rates and the discount rate (Winograd / Kaminitz)**

- The Winograd committee (ועדת וינוגרד) cut the discount rate used to capitalise future losses from 3% to 2% (regulations published in 2016, effective October 2017 per press), raising lump-sum bodily-injury awards; large compulsory writers (Menora, Harel, Phoenix, Direct Insurance) set aside hundreds of millions of shekels and some raised compulsory tariffs by more than 10%; in January 2019 the Kaminitz committee (ועדת קמיניץ) recommended restoring 3% and the industry expected ~8% cuts in compulsory prices — [Calcalist, 15 Jan 2019](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3754243,00.html) [PR]. The courts subsequently kept the discount rate at 3% — [hon.co.il](https://www.hon.co.il/?p=115011) [SE; judgment not identified]. Menbara uses a 3% discount assumption for National Insurance claims — [Menbara 2024](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2024.pdf) [PR].
- Market interest rates: higher rates in 2023 cut the present value of compulsory liabilities and helped turn the line from loss to profit — [Globes, 14 Dec 2023](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001465106) [PR]; in 2026 a lower yield curve and higher CPI increased liabilities, "mainly in compulsory motor", offsetting investment gains at Libra — [Bizportal, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.bizportal.co.il/capitalmarket/news/article/20040210) [PR]; Direct Insurance's insurance finance expenses rose to NIS 38.8m in Q3 2025 from NIS 23m because of the curve — [Bizportal, 25 Nov 2025](https://www.bizportal.co.il/capitalmarket/news/article/20024203) [PR].

**Other**

- Reinsurance: an industry source says large reinsurers such as Swiss Re left the Israeli market, so insurers pay more to those remaining, causing some losses in compulsory — [Globes, 6 Jan 2026](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001531135) [PR, single unnamed source].
- Mileage: Menbara finds claim cost of about NIS 70–75 per 1,000 km for private cars, taxis, trucks and minibuses (NIS 47 for company-owned private cars) and recommends adding mileage as a rating variable — [Menbara 2022](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2022.pd_.pdf) [PR]. War-related drops in driving reduced claims in late 2023, Q4 2024 and early 2026 — [CMA Report 2024 ch. 3](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/news/press_0083/he/Commisioner%20Report%20Design%20B_chapter3%20ver%206.0_digital.pdf) [PR]; [Globes, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001516240) [PR].
- Fraud: only dated estimates were found — at least NIS 200m a year in car and property insurance (a software vendor's estimate from the 2000s) and a 1996 study putting fraud at ~20% of premiums — [Bizportal archive](https://www.bizportal.co.il/general/news/article/105567) [SE].

### Inferences
- Theft cost of ~NIS 963m in 2023 equals about 8% of that year's motor property premiums (NIS 12.15bn) and roughly 11% of claims; with ~15,400 thefts in 2025 and ~12,500 annualised in H1 2026, the theft burden has fallen by roughly a third from the 2024 peak, which is consistent with the 25–29% fall in comprehensive prices [INF].
- The list-price appraisal rule is an Israel-specific regulatory cost driver that insurers can only partly neutralise through arrangement garages; it is still in force in November 2026 [INF].
- High purchase tax inflates insured values and therefore theft and total-loss payouts and the value-related part of comprehensive premiums, but no source quantifies the effect on premiums [INF].
- For compulsory cover the dominant drivers are macro (wages, CPI, interest rates) and legal (statutory transfers), not driving behaviour: claim frequency has fallen for a decade [INF].

### Gaps
- No consistent official annual theft series 2022–2026 was found; police, Knesset and tracking-company figures use different definitions and conflict.
- No recent quantified estimate of motor insurance fraud or of legal/litigation costs in Israel.
- No data on the share of repairs done in arrangement garages after 2023, or on average repair cost per claim by brand (Chinese vs others) from a primary source.
- Effect of purchase tax on premiums is asserted in the press but not measured.

## 5. Accident and claims statistics

### Takeaway
Road deaths hit a 19-year high of 459 in 2025 (4.6 per 100,000 residents; 6.5 per billion vehicle-km in 2024) even as police-recorded injury accidents fell; 2026 is running slightly lower (341 deaths to end-September vs 352). ### Inferences
- Comprehensive-policy claim frequency ≈ 12.9 per 100 insured vehicles in 2019 (270k ÷ 2,089k), 13.2 in 2022, 12.9 in 2023, 12.8 in 2024 and 12.7 in 2025; TP-only ≈ 9–11 per 100 [INF].
- Average motor property claim ≈ NIS 21,600 in 2025 (NIS 9bn ÷ 416,000 claims); claims cost per insured vehicle ≈ NIS 2,530 against an average premium of ≈ NIS 3,956, i.e. 64%, matching the official loss ratio [INF].
- Compulsory: about 1.1–1.4 claim events per 100 private cars per year at ~NIS 85,000–90,000 each ≈ NIS 1,150–1,270 of claims per car per year; that is roughly the whole net premium, which is why the line depends on investment income [INF].
- 2025 combined record deaths with fewer recorded injury accidents; because compulsory cost is driven by a small number of severe claims, falling minor-injury counts do not translate into lower compulsory cost [INF].
- Deaths per 100,000 for 2019–2024 can only be bracketed from the sources read: ~4.0 average for 2022–2024 and 4.6 in 2025 (official); the brief's per-year series was not found [INF].

### Gaps
- Annual counts of injured and seriously injured for each year 2019–2024, and deaths per 100,000 by year, were not retrieved (only 2024–2026 CBS/RSA figures were read); vehicle-km for 2025 is not yet published.
- The CBS "expanded" accident counts and the "−26%" for 2025 quoted by Bizportal look internally inconsistent with the −8.2% fall in casualties and were not checked against the CBS release.
- No official average claim severity for motor property is published; the NIS 21,600 above is my estimate from two different CMA numbers (payments and claims handled).
- No published split of motor property claims into accident, theft and other perils, apart from the "~15% theft" press figure.

## 6. Market structure and competition

### Takeaway
On paper motor insurance is unconcentrated (HHI 0.08–0.09; 16–17 writers; the top five hold 54–57%), and the regulator calls it "one of the most competitive markets in the economy"; in practice eight insurers with ~75% of motor property were found in 2025 to be charging above risk, list tariffs are approved by the regulator as ceilings while real prices are set by discretionary discounts, agents still take ~10% of general-insurance premiums at the traditional insurers, and the main price-comparison platforms are now owned by insurers.

**Tariff regulation**

- Motor property: "the CMA approves the maximum tariff each insurer may charge … there is no fixed date; an insurer files when it chooses, once a year or once in several years"; the customer price is the ceiling minus discounts (insurance history, security devices), and most of the 2021–2024 price surge came from shrinking discounts, not new approvals. The CMA's power to order tariffs down is described as unusual internationally — [Calcalist, 27 Nov 2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/rkbh00hnbwl) [PR]. An industry source calls the regulator's order "a threat without teeth" for that reason — [Globes, 6 Jan 2026](https://www.globes.co.il/news/article.aspx?did=1001531135) [PR].
- Compulsory: opened to "controlled competition" by a 1997 law (uniform supervised tariff before that, through the Avner pool); tariffs are set by insurers but must rest on variables approved by the Commissioner and statistical analysis, and need approval; the CMA "in certain cases, especially compulsory, does not approve tariff increases" — [CMA Report 2025 ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf) [PR]; [Calcalist, 27 Nov 2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/rkbh00hnbwl) [PR]; [State Comptroller report](https://library.mevaker.gov.il/sites/DigitalLibrary/Documents/60b/2010-60b-207-Rechev.pdf) [SE]; [Hamichlol encyclopedia entry](https://www.hamichlol.org.il/%D7%91%D7%99%D7%98%D7%95%D7%97_%D7%A6%D7%93_%D7%92%27) [SE]. The CMA approved compulsory increases in late 2022 and 2023 (tariffs ~12% higher in 2023 than 2022) on Menbara's recommendation — [Menbara 2022](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2022.pd_.pdf) [PR].
- Permitted compulsory rating variables: vehicle — ownership (private/company), engine volume, airbags, ESP, ABS, forward-collision and lane-departure warning systems; driver — age, driving seniority, sex, number of accidents, number of licence suspensions — [Bizportal guide, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.bizportal.co.il/guides/news/article/20038739) [PR]. Menbara recommends adding mileage and "intervening safety systems" and notes the sex variable is no longer statistically reliable because the share of declared young female drivers jumped from 41% to 62% after a discount was introduced — [Menbara 2022, ch. 20](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/statistical_autoinsurance_publishments/he/MBI-Recommendations-Report-2022.pd_.pdf) [PR].

### Inferences
- Insurers that sell directly (Direct Insurance, AIG, Libra, WeSure) held about 26.7% of motor property premiums and 22.8% of compulsory premiums in 2025 (sum of the CMA shares), so direct distribution is far larger in motor than the ~10% reported for general insurance as a whole in 2022 [INF].
- At the agent-based insurers roughly 11–14% of general-insurance premium goes to commissions versus under 2% at the direct writers; on a NIS 3,950 average motor property policy that is about NIS 430–550 of distribution cost [INF, applying company-wide general-insurance rates to motor].
- Low measured concentration coexisted with a market-wide price rise the regulator judged excessive at eight of nine insurers examined; the mechanism was opaque discounting under approved ceilings rather than concentration [INF].
- Ownership of Wobi, Besti and Polisa by insurers weakens independent price comparison just as prices fall [INF].

### Gaps
- No data on churn or renewal switching rates; sources conflict anecdotally ("almost no real loyalty" vs "most drivers renew with the same company without checking", both Bizportal).
- No motor-specific split of sales by channel (agent, direct, digital) and no motor-specific commission rate.
- Usage statistics for the CMA's compulsory calculator and "Har HaBituach" were not found; the gov.il calculator pages could not be opened.

## 7. What the regulator said and did about motor prices and profits, 2024–2026

### Takeaway
For the first time the CMA used its power to force tariffs down: after a review opened in September 2024 it found "material gaps" between premiums and risk, in November 2025 ordered eight insurers to file lower comprehensive and third-party tariffs by 26 February 2026 and get approval by 30 April 2026 on pain of a sales ban, briefly barred Direct Insurance on 1 May 2026, and completed the process in 2026; it left compulsory tariffs alone apart from the Pool.

### Cited Findings
- 2023: Commissioner's ruling allowing insurers to reduce payouts by the gap between list and market parts prices for repairs outside arrangement garages — [CMA Report 2025 ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/tender-0102/he/Commissioner%20Report%202025%20chapter%205_Digital%20-%20%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%20%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A4%D7%99.pdf) [PR]; [Calcalist, 27 Nov 2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/rkbh00hnbwl) [PR].
- September 2024: the CMA demanded multi-year pricing data from all 14 motor insurers; the press reported it was examining motor profitability after the five largest insurers posted NIS 523m of motor underwriting profit in H1 2024. Before that it had reviewed compulsory data and concluded motorcycle compulsory risk tariffs allowed a ~7% cut. It had so far avoided heavy intervention for fear of driving unprofitable insurers out, as happened in long-term-care insurance in 2019 — [Calcalist, 22 Sep 2024](https://www.calcalist.co.il/investing/article/sjbmfsa6r) [PR].
- CMA annual report for 2024 (published September 2025): "in view of the downward trend in vehicle thefts and claim costs in motor property in 2024, the Authority decided to examine the fit between premiums and the insured's risk, in order to consider reducing premiums" — [CMA Report 2024 ch. 5](https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/report_0010/he/Commisioner%20Report%20Design%20B_chapter5_%20ver%206.0_digital.pdf) [PR].
- April 2025: letter to nine insurers (Direct Insurance, Phoenix, Harel, Clal, Menora, Shlomo, Shomera, Ayalon, Migdal) to explain tariffs: "in some companies there is a considerable gap between claim cost and inherent risk and the premium actually collected" — [Calcalist, 23 Apr 2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/car/article/byfsxmu1ge) [PR].
- July 2025: financial sanctions of NIS 125,000 each on Phoenix and Direct Insurance for late and omitted data (including data from the first months after the war began, when claims and thefts dropped), and a conditional sanction on WeSure; Direct Insurance's was later halved — [Calcalist, 8 Dec 2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/b13mcsqz11l) [PR].

- Gal, December 2025: "We started the examination before the first year of exceptional profitability had even ended … we made clear to the insurers that if we did not see price falls we would intervene … 2024 was a year of high profitability in motor insurance and since the start of this year profitability has remained very high … The big question is whether the fall in claim costs is passed on to customers" — [Calcalist, 8 Dec 2025](https://www.calcalist.co.il/local_news/article/b13mcsqz11l) [PR].

### Inferences
- The regulator's own words ("exceptional profitability", "material gaps between risk and premium", eight of nine insurers over-charging) are the strongest official support for the claim that comprehensive and third-party cover was over-priced in 2024–2025; they do not extend to compulsory cover, where the regulator has been approving increases roughly in line with CPI [INF].
- The sequence of evidence (thefts and claims falling from 2024, loss ratio 66% then 64%, premiums still rising in 2024) shows prices lagged costs by about a year on the way down, after insurers had lost money for two years on the way up [INF].
- By autumn 2026 the correction is visible in both prices (−25–29% from peak for comprehensive) and profits (double-digit declines in Q2 2026 motor profit, one large insurer in loss), so a snapshot of 2024–2025 profitability overstates current margins [INF].

### Gaps
- The text of the Commissioner's November 2025 letter and any written rule capping the motor profit margin were not found; the "single-digit margin" claim rests on one press article.

- No CMA statement comparing Israeli motor prices or profits with other countries was found; the only international comparison located is the Knesset Research Center's index comparison with the EU (+5% real vs +1.9%, January 2019–October 2025).
